# 1. Instructions for continuation

Use this document as the current authority for faction work. Do not silently blend it with superseded ideas.

- Do not invent proper faction names. Use the established descriptive labels.
- Design factions one at a time as complete RTS factions. Do not substitute a unit list or implementation sketch for a complete faction design.
- Balance all factions at the neutral systemic level. Scenario weather, scripted events, and the main campaign's growing Cthulhu advantage may then deliberately disturb that balance.
- Preserve three sharply different allegiance pressures: Michael recruits adult women through personal attraction and relationship conditions; Cthulhu converts statistically through madness and deterministically through unresolved death; pirates hire dissatisfied living people with gold, shares, and opportunity.
- Pirate recruitment may affect living men and women from other factions when they are sufficiently dissatisfied and can be contacted, but Michael-aligned women never leave him and therefore cannot be hired by pirates.
- Preserve the distinction between pirate recruitment and pirate capture. A prisoner does not become a pirate merely by being captured; recruitment requires a separate voluntary paid agreement.
- Let pirates seize and reuse all practical categories of portable equipment, vessels, and machinery. This is asset seizure rather than population recruitment and makes pirates a deliberate counter to equipment- and machine-heavy factions, especially an overextended Michael faction.
- Fox women may woo living men from other factions. This is a distributed relationship system attached to individual fox women, not a centralized leader power and not a gold transaction.
- A man wooed into the fox faction retains his identity and source-faction abilities. If the fox woman later joins Michael, the man does not automatically follow her into Michael's faction.
- Elves begin few in number and possess a very slow, tightly limited native population-replacement engine. Their strength comes from magic, craft, hardiness, preparation, and preservation rather than population throughput.
- Do not create a protected or permanently immune class of female characters. Every adult female character is potentially recruitable by Michael under achievable conditions.
- Do not make every female NPC a bespoke romance production. Use a continuous NPC population containing ordinary generated units, emergent heroes, and authored notables.
- Do not create four predetermined special companion women. Michael chooses up to four women from the wider female NPC population for his main harem and party.
- Preserve the distinction between recruitment into Michael's faction and selection for the main harem.
- Preserve permanent living allegiance after a woman joins Michael.
- Preserve the global midnight rule: dead characters not restored in time rise for Cthulhu.
- Preserve character identity through faction transfer, death, undeath, recovery, and resurrection.
- Do not give all factions unit-stealing mechanics. Their strengths must arise from distinct economic and battlefield systems.
- Keep the five-person party and the autonomous RTS layer connected without turning Michael into a conventional overhead unit-micromanagement commander.
- Label new recommendations **Provisional** until the user approves them.
- Require **complete asymmetric coolness** from every faction and race. Shared weapons or necessary roles never justify a shared production loop, movement doctrine, tactical rhythm, territorial method, decision pattern, spectacle, failure story, or victory fantasy.
- Treat the eleven installed character photographs as production identity anchors. Do not replace, redraw, or publish them without a new approval pass.
- Preserve the hero-sheet boundary: concept and narrative, one party passive, one strategic passive, four encounter powers, and one ultimate.
- Do not put recruitment, research, forecasts, route planning, production bonuses, or other strategic functions into encounter-power slots.

# 2. Established game and faction context

Pirate Island is a character-scale RPG taking place inside a living autonomous RTS simulation.

The island is a complex network graph, not a three-lane map. One graph node represents one playable room or tactical location. Routes connect settlements, resources, ports, wilderness, dungeons, strategic structures, and faction fronts.

The island contains six factions:

1. Asian fox people.
2. Colonial powers.
3. Pirates.
4. Elves.
5. Cthulhu faction.
6. Captain Michael's player-created faction.

The first five exist when the campaign begins. Captain Michael begins alone and creates the sixth during play.

Each faction acts autonomously. Its AI evaluates survival, territory, buildings, population, morale, resources, supply, relationships, grudges, opportunities, enemies, alliances, victory conditions, weather, and its current understanding of Cthulhu. Factions build, upgrade, spawn, train, patrol, attack, defend, trade, recover, retreat, ally, betray, and pursue strategic goals without waiting for the player.

The player experiences this simulation through Captain Michael and up to four women in his main harem. The mature main party therefore contains five principal characters, but Michael begins the campaign alone and may operate with fewer than four companions while the party is forming.

The player may pause the game normally. The strategic layer should feel like a living island that can be influenced, not a hostile real-time alarm system that punishes the player for reading, talking, or thinking.

The game permits organic paths, mixed outcomes, multiple forms of victory, and genuine defeat. A very long campaign can end in failure. The campaign is not guaranteed to preserve every faction, every important character, or the player's preferred political outcome.

# 3. Population is made of persistent units

Faction populations are not merely colored manpower totals. Their organic units are persistent people.

Every persistent person has at least:

- A stable identity.
- A name.
- A sex or recruitment-category identity relevant to faction mechanics.
- A current faction.
- A faction of origin.
- A current role.
- A building or place of origin when generated by the simulation.
- Combat and noncombat capabilities.
- Morale.
- Loyalty.
- Relationships.
- Relevant memories and history.
- A living, dead, undead, corrupted, or restored state.

The population contains men and women at multiple levels of importance.

## 3.1 Ordinary generated units

Ordinary workers, fighters, sailors, scouts, traders, technicians, practitioners, and other units are generated by faction buildings. They receive names and lightweight generated backstories. They immediately participate in the RTS simulation.

An ordinary unit may:

- Work in or support a building.
- Carry resources.
- Operate a trade or transport route.
- Join an attack or defense.
- Patrol.
- Scout.
- Repair.
- Serve as a vendor.
- Move with a refugee group.
- Gain experience.
- Form relationships.
- Become dissatisfied.
- Rebel.
- Be recruited through one of the two special recruitment systems.
- Become an emergent hero.
- Die.
- Be resurrected or restored.
- Return for Cthulhu at midnight.

Ordinary does not mean disposable. It describes content depth and starting strategic importance, not whether the character persists.

## 3.2 Hero units

Some faction units are heroes. A hero is an unusually capable, strategically important unit rather than a member of a separate romance caste.

A hero may be:

- An authored notable.
- A faction leader.
- A military commander.
- A powerful fighter.
- A magical practitioner.
- An engineer.
- A political figure.
- A spy.
- A famous sailor or explorer.
- A normal generated unit promoted through survival, achievement, relationships, or faction need.

Heroes may possess unique abilities, superior statistics, command influence, special equipment, faction authority, larger relationship networks, more authored content, and more consequential death or defection states.

Hero units are not balanced against normal units. A hero is allowed to be plainly stronger and generally makes a better main-party choice.

## 3.3 Authored notables

Each faction also has recurring named men and women with authored identities, relationships, political roles, and story functions. Some are heroes; others may be important because of information, family, romance, trade, religion, faction legitimacy, or connection to a companion.

Notables remain part of the simulation. They work, travel, command, negotiate, fight, suffer morale changes, change allegiance where their faction rules permit, die, and return as undead if not restored. They do not wait outside the simulation for a player-authored scene.

## 3.4 Promotion and emergent importance

A generated unit can become notable through play. Repeated survival, promotion, a relationship with a hero, a dramatic rebellion, a remarkable battle, a rescue, or selection for Michael's main harem can elevate an otherwise ordinary person into the recurring cast.

The game should remember that history. It should not replace the unit with an unrelated bespoke version.

# 4. Buildings and unit generation

Buildings are the source of ordinary RTS population cycles only where a faction's own rules permit population generation. Colonials, fox people, pirates, and elves use buildings for radically different forms of native generation. Michael does not generate organic followers from buildings. Elven generation exists but is the slowest and most limited native cycle on the island.

Examples include:

- Residences producing workers, settlers, and civilian specialists.
- Barracks training fighters.
- Ports producing sailors and dockworkers.
- Markets producing merchants and service workers.
- Workshops producing craftspeople and engineers.
- Magical structures producing practitioners or attendants.
- Religious structures producing clergy or ritual workers.
- Administrative buildings producing officials.
- Faction-specific buildings producing faction-specific people, creatures, pets, or other units.

The building represents the social, logistical, and training structure that introduces a person into the persistent simulation. It does not require a literal depiction of instantaneous adulthood.

Building identity and level may influence:

- Spawn rate.
- Available professions.
- Starting equipment.
- Training quality.
- Minimum and maximum capability.
- Chance of producing specialists.
- Chance of producing a hero-quality unit.
- Faction loyalty.
- Backstory components.
- Relationship seeds.
- Magical or technological traits.

This connects population quality to the evolving RTS board. Where a faction can generate people, a mature building can produce more dangerous defenders and more valuable potential recruits. Destroying or capturing it changes both military production and the future character pool. Elven structures primarily improve, equip, conceal, heal, and preserve existing elves while generating replacements only through a deliberately slow and narrow cycle.

Captain Michael is one exception. His industry manufactures machines, equipment, transport, infrastructure, and military capacity. He begins without an organic population and gains adult organic members by recruiting women who already exist elsewhere on the island. His residences and institutions may house, organize, train, heal, and equip recruited women, but his early faction identity must not collapse into a normal house timer that manufactures followers.

The elves are a constrained exception rather than a zero-generation faction. Their starting population is small, and suitable sanctuaries can eventually generate new elves, but not quickly enough to answer ordinary attrition or emergency battlefield losses. Their buildings chiefly multiply capability, survivability, magical reach, craft quality, concealment, and recovery. Resurrection returns an existing identity and is faster than waiting for a replacement cycle, so recovering individual elves remains strategically critical.

The exact long-horizon rules for births, immigration, or later civilian growth inside a mature Michael settlement remain unresolved. They must not erase his solo start or his recruitment-based founding identity.

# 5. Captain Michael's complete faction direction

## 5.1 Founding state

Captain Michael begins as a shipwreck survivor and a literal one-person faction.

He has:

- No soldiers.
- No workers.
- No settlers.
- No assembled companions.
- No inherited territory.
- No established state.
- No functioning industrial base.
- No ordinary organic recruitment building.

He has his knowledge, his personal capabilities, the remains of the shipwreck, and enough salvage or hand equipment to begin the first bootstrap cycle.

The intended founding sequence is:

> Michael alone -> salvage and first apparatus -> mechanical labor -> primitive workshop -> additional machinery -> defensible foothold -> recruited women -> functioning faction

The player does not inherit a kingdom. The faction visibly comes into existence around Michael.

## 5.2 The faction's two defining advantages

Captain Michael has two defining advantages as faction leader.

### Technological supremacy

Michael possesses an extraordinary alternate-1870s technological lead. His industry produces distinctive mechanical unit types and infrastructure that no other faction can match directly.

### Female recruitment

Michael may recruit any adult female character on the island under the right achievable conditions. This includes ordinary units, heroes, leaders, rebels, cultists, madness-converted women, and appropriate undead women.

All other faction systems support the expression of these two advantages. Industry, territory, logistics, diplomacy, personal relationships, and player direction are necessary, but they are not additional defining gimmicks.

## 5.3 Technological language

Michael's technology is fantastically advanced for an alternate 1870s imagination. It is not modern hardware disguised with brass decoration.

The visual and functional language includes:

- Steam as the principal motive force.
- Boilers, pistons, valves, gears, flywheels, pressure vessels, articulated limbs, and mechanical linkages.
- Gas used for illumination, heat, ignition, lift, and industrial processes.
- Electricity used in support of signaling, instruments, ignition, communication, experimentation, and specialized control.
- Mechanical forms derived from animals, cavalry, wagons, ships, artillery, cranes, and machinery familiar to the period.
- Unguided rockets affected by weather, visibility, preparation, and storage.
- Airships dependent on lift, weather, fuel, crews, moorings, and vulnerable ground facilities.
- Steam wagons and traction-engine logic rather than modern cars.

Captain Michael has far more technology than the other factions, but the technology must remain legible as 1870s speculative engineering.

## 5.4 Economy and industrial loop

The faction requires a real RTS economy. Its likely resource and capacity families are:

- Provisions.
- Fresh water.
- Fuel.
- Raw materials.
- Precision parts.
- Ammunition or military supply.
- Expertise as a capacity.
- Command as a capacity.

The industrial loop is:

> salvage and resources -> water and fuel -> workshop capacity -> precision parts -> machines and buildings -> extraction and transport -> connected depots and routes -> larger industrial reach

Michael's faction is strongest around functioning infrastructure. Its advanced machines do not remove the need for fuel, water, repair, routes, skilled personnel, and spare parts.

## 5.5 Buildings

The current building family includes:

- Core workshop.
- Salvage yard.
- Foundry or material works.
- Precision machine works.
- Boiler and power works.
- Automata works.
- Wagon and transport yard.
- Heavy engineering works.
- Rocket works and magazine.
- Airship mast and air works.
- Signal and observation station.
- Quarters and training grounds for recruited personnel.
- Medical and recovery facility.
- Resurrection machinery when acquired or developed.
- Defensive works.
- Depots for fuel, water, ammunition, and parts.

All structures must use standard footprints and clearance envelopes so generated layouts remain navigable and collision-safe. Exact standard dimensions remain unresolved.

## 5.6 Machine roster

Michael's industrial population supplies scalable labor and force through machines.

### Utility automata

Construction, salvage, repair, hauling, basic facility work, and hazardous-duty labor.

### Mechanical dogs

Patrol, warning, scouting, tracking, escort, and pursuit of small threats.

### Mechanical cavalry

Fast response, reconnaissance, pursuit, courier work, route control, and mobile screening.

### Mechanical bears

Heavy labor, breaching, close defense, anti-monster combat, and protection of important personnel.

### Mechanical elephants

Heavy transport, artillery movement, crane work, siege support, and mobile command or repair functions.

### Steam wagons

The logistical backbone: personnel transport, fuel, water, ammunition, salvage, workshops, and evacuation.

### Walkers

Specialized construction, heavy combat, siege, and traversal. They are major machines rather than the default form of every unit.

### Rockets

Signaling, illumination, bombardment, fire, area denial, and siege. They remain unguided and sensitive to storage, wind, visibility, and preparation.

### Steam airships

Reconnaissance, observation, specialist transport, emergency supply, evacuation, and limited strategic projection. They depend on weather, fuel or lift systems, crews, and mooring infrastructure.

## 5.7 Combined-arms identity

Machines provide:

- Scale.
- Labor.
- Construction.
- Transport.
- Armor.
- Firepower.
- Hazard tolerance.
- Repairable military mass.

Recruited women provide:

- Judgment.
- Leadership.
- Local knowledge.
- Culture.
- Magic.
- Diplomacy.
- Intelligence.
- Romance and relationships.
- Origin-faction specialties.
- Human improvisation.

The most characteristic Michael units combine both halves: a woman retaining the skills and appearance of her faction of origin while operating with Michael's equipment, transport, communications, weapons, and mechanical support.

## 5.8 Territory and logistics

Michael controls territory through connected infrastructure rather than pure population density.

His faction establishes:

- Workshops.
- Depots.
- Roads and improved routes.
- Bridges.
- Signals.
- Patrols.
- Fuel and water access.
- Repair coverage.
- Transport schedules.
- Defensive machinery.

Prepared routes make the faction fast and formidable. Broken bridges, cut depots, mud, fuel shortages, water shortages, destroyed precision works, and disconnected operations expose its weaknesses.

## 5.9 Strategic direction

The faction operates mostly automatically but remains easy for the player to influence.

The player supplies high-level directives such as:

- Defend.
- Develop.
- Attack.
- Support.
- Avoid.
- Withdraw.
- Evacuate.
- Salvage.
- Escort.
- Reinforce.
- Observe.
- Recruit.
- Repair.

The faction AI handles pathing, routine staffing, supply, patrols, production, maintenance, and local responses. Michael does not select and micromanage every unit as an overhead RTS commander.

Major wars, treaty breaking, principal relationship commitments, irreplaceable resource expenditure, evacuation of the strategic core, final confrontation, and explicit victory attempts should require player involvement.

## 5.10 Strategic AI priorities

Michael's faction generally prioritizes:

1. Preserve Michael and any viable faction-recovery chain.
2. Preserve the industrial core.
3. Preserve water, fuel, and precision production.
4. Protect irreplaceable women and specialists.
5. Maintain connected supply.
6. Recover bodies before midnight.
7. Repair valuable machinery.
8. Execute player directives.
9. Convert resources into infrastructure and machines.
10. Pursue female recruitment opportunities.
11. Deny threatening rival resources.
12. Pursue the player's approved victory route.

## 5.11 Strengths

- Highest technological ceiling.
- Distinctive mechanical roster.
- Machine-based force multiplication.
- Strong construction, repair, transport, and salvage.
- Powerful prepared-route logistics.
- Fortified territorial control.
- Siege and anti-monster potential.
- Access to female units and heroes from every faction.
- Mixed-origin organic abilities.
- Very high responsiveness to player strategy.
- Strong late-game flexibility.

## 5.12 Weaknesses

- Starts as one person.
- No native opening population.
- Severe early vulnerability.
- Industrial bottlenecks.
- Dependence on water, fuel, routes, precision production, and repair.
- Low initial magic unless recruited.
- Irreplaceable organic losses.
- Smaller organic population than established factions.
- Weather sensitivity, especially for rockets, roads, and airships.
- Political consequences when recruiting valuable women.
- Cthulhu gains any dead personnel not recovered before midnight.

## 5.13 Elimination and recovery

Loss of one base does not automatically eliminate Michael's faction. It remains viable while a credible recovery chain survives through some combination of:

- Michael.
- Recruited leadership.
- A core or mobile workshop.
- Salvage.
- Tools and parts.
- Fuel and water.
- A valid rebuilding site.
- Allied refuge.
- A protecting force.

The faction is eliminated when no viable recovery chain remains. Exact loss and restoration behavior if Michael himself dies remains unresolved.

## 5.14 Captain Michael as a hero character

### Accepted character center

**Captain Michael - The One-Man Industrial Revolution**

Michael is a steampunk gunslinger, gentleman adventurer, shipwrecked inventor, romantic faction founder, and dangerously confident revolutionary. He is a clearly adult young man of approximately twenty to twenty-three, not a mature established patriarch. He has already worked among Argentines and in Texas, crossed oceans, survived the loss of his ship, and arrived with ideas about machines and government that are just barely imaginable in the alternate 1870s and far beyond anything else on the island.

He begins with a revolver, cavalry saber, tool roll, damaged machinery, salvage, and the conviction that a country can be built from wreckage if he remains alive long enough. His personal fantasy and faction fantasy are the same continuous arc:

> Start with one young man, one gun, one sword, and a wreck. End with mechanical cavalry, steam walkers, rockets, airships, formidable women from across the island, and a country that did not exist before he arrived.

Michael is charming, commanding, ingenious, romantic, and dangerous. He believes people should be free, but he also tends to believe that he is the person best qualified to determine what freedom presently requires. That contradiction must drive party arguments, romance, political choices, and the possible success or failure of his state. His inventions can save the island while concentrating extraordinary military power around one person.

Female recruitment is a relationship and faction-transfer system. It must not consume one of Michael's four encounter powers. His eventual combat kit must make him a complete mobile gunfighter-engineer even in a room containing no recruitable woman.

### Approved character photograph

![Captain Michael - approved character photograph](assets/characters/captain-michael.png)

This image is the current production identity anchor for Captain Michael.

The following elements are locked for downstream portraits, models, and promotional character presentations:

- Clearly adult apparent age of approximately twenty to twenty-three.
- Slim, lean, wiry adventurer build rather than broad or heavily muscular proportions.
- Youthful face, dark curls, light stubble, confident gaze, and romantic-dangerous charisma.
- Broad black frontier hat.
- Long dark weathered captain's coat.
- Open cream shirt, fitted navy waistcoat, and red Argentine-style gaucho sash.
- Dark fitted trousers, practical tall boots, gloves, tool belt, revolver, and cavalry saber.
- Alternate-1870s equipment rather than modern hardware covered in brass decoration.
- The visual ability to read simultaneously as captain, gunfighter, swordsman, inventor, and founder.

The shipwreck and storm-breaking light establish his campaign-opening context. They are not required in every later portrait.

### Integrated steampunk equipment contract

Michael's personal apparatus must read as equipment he can actually wear while running, climbing, shooting, and fencing.

The approved system consists of:

- One compact brass-and-blued-steel steam power unit seated close behind his shoulder blades.
- A fitted dark-leather load-bearing harness transferring weight through the shoulders and waist.
- A consolidated pressure gauge, boiler, valves, and fittings rather than unrelated decorative mechanisms.
- A reinforced steam hose and protected linkages following one real arm.
- A compact piston-assisted forearm bracer supporting the wrist and elbow.
- Visible anchors into the shoulder harness, coat construction, belt, and bracer.
- Exactly two human arms and two hands.

Rejected personal-equipment readings include a loose auxiliary claw, an unexplained third arm, machinery that floats beside him, a giant mechanical hand, an oversized decorative backpack, and components that have no visible load path or connection.

### Mechanical status

Michael's hero identity and character photograph are accepted. His party passive, strategic passive, four encounter powers, ultimate, resource loop, and counterplay remain to be completed as one coherent hero kit. The kit should descend from the mobile gunfighter-engineer fantasy rather than treating his faction systems as combat buttons.

# 6. Michael's universal female recruitment rule

The rule is binary by target category:

- **Adult female character:** potentially recruitable by Michael.
- **Male character:** cannot be recruited by Michael.
- **Non-female or non-person unit:** cannot be recruited by Michael.

There is no protected female category and no permanent female immunity.

Every adult female person remains within Michael's recruitment domain, including:

- Ordinary workers.
- Ordinary fighters.
- Specialists.
- Officers.
- Heroes.
- Faction leaders.
- Rebels.
- Refugees.
- Prisoners.
- Living cultists.
- Madness-converted women.
- Corrupted living women.
- Sapient undead women.
- Former Michael women raised by Cthulhu.
- A formerly mindless undead woman if enough of her original identity is restored for her to function as a person again.

Recruitment may be easy or difficult. Difficulty comes from the actual woman and situation:

- Loyalty.
- Attraction.
- Interest in Michael's technology.
- Morale.
- Ambition.
- Personal relationships.
- Duties.
- Location.
- Faction politics.
- War.
- Madness.
- Undeath.
- Memory loss.
- Access.
- The need for an artifact, rescue, victory, cure, or other intervention.

An artifact or quest changes the situation; it does not unlock a previously immune class of women.

Other factions may move, guard, reward, isolate, or persuade valuable women. These actions can make contact and recruitment difficult, but they cannot switch off Michael's ability. The design must never return to language describing female units as permanently protected from him.

Michael cannot affect male units through this ability. He also cannot use it on animals, pets, dinosaurs, machines, buildings, environmental corruption, summoned non-person creatures, or monsters without an individual female person remaining.

## 6.1 Recruitment transfers real power

A recruited woman is removed from her previous faction and added to Michael's faction.

Her former faction loses her:

- Labor.
- Combat power.
- Command.
- Magic.
- Expertise.
- Intelligence.
- Relationships.
- Political influence.
- Hero abilities.
- Contribution to buildings, routes, formations, and objectives.

Michael gains those capabilities.

The strategic and diplomatic consequences depend on the woman and the circumstances. Recruitment may anger or please her former faction.

Potentially favorable circumstances include:

- An agreed transfer.
- An alliance arrangement.
- Michael rescuing or resurrecting her when her faction could not.
- Her becoming a liaison.
- Technology or other compensation.
- Her departure solving an internal political problem.
- Her position in Michael's faction giving her former people useful influence.

Potentially hostile circumstances include:

- Secretly wooing away a loyal specialist.
- Taking a commander during war.
- Causing a military unit or settlement to collapse.
- Receiving stolen equipment or intelligence with her.
- Humiliating a faction leader.
- Repeatedly taking a faction's best women.
- Violating a treaty or hospitality.

The former faction's official reaction may differ from the reactions of her family, rivals, friends, subordinates, and enemies.

## 6.2 Ease and frequency

Michael's recruitment power must occur frequently enough to function as a faction pillar. Ordinary female units should not all require major authored courtship campaigns.

A normal woman may join after contact, a favorable event, a short generated personal quest, or a straightforward decision. Hero units and faction leaders may require more substantial circumstances because their lives and strategic roles are more complicated, not because heroes are categorically protected.

# 7. Permanent allegiance inside Michael's faction

Once a woman willingly joins Michael's faction, she does not defect to another living faction.

She cannot be taken away through:

- Ordinary morale collapse.
- Bribery.
- Propaganda.
- Another faction's diplomacy.
- A request from her former leader.
- A conventional rebellion transfer.
- Cthulhu's living madness-recruitment endpoint.

Her decision to join Michael is durable.

This does not make her an obedient machine. She may still:

- Disagree with Michael.
- Become unhappy.
- Argue with other women.
- Refuse a specific request.
- Demand change.
- Lose morale or efficiency.
- Suffer fear or madness symptoms.
- Pursue independent objectives.
- Require relationship work.
- Withdraw temporarily from a role.
- Create internal political pressure.

What she does not do while alive is change faction allegiance away from Michael.

Death is the exception. An unrecovered dead woman can be raised by Cthulhu at midnight and temporarily become a Cthulhu unit.

# 8. Main harem and five-character party

Michael can have four women in his main harem. Together they form the principal five-character party:

> Captain Michael + four women

There is no predetermined class of four special companion women. The four may be selected from the wider female NPC pool.

Any suitable recruited adult woman may potentially occupy a main-harem slot:

- An authored hero.
- A faction leader.
- A recurring notable.
- A generated worker.
- A generated fighter.
- An emergent hero.
- A former rebel.
- A madness-recovered woman.
- A sapient undead woman.
- A woman restored after Cthulhu claimed her.

Recruitment into Michael's faction does not automatically place a woman in the main harem. The faction may contain many women while only four occupy the central romantic and adventuring positions.

## 8.1 Unequal candidates

Women are not balanced against one another as party choices.

Some are plainly stronger because they possess:

- Better combat ability.
- Unique powers.
- Magic.
- Leadership.
- Strategic command.
- Political influence.
- Technical talent.
- Intelligence access.
- Better growth potential.
- More authored quests and dialogue.

A hero woman will commonly be a stronger main-harem choice than a normal generated woman. The design does not need to invent compensating superpowers for every weaker candidate.

Other considerations may still make a mechanically weaker woman attractive:

- Immediate availability.
- Loyalty and compatibility.
- A relationship the player values.
- Low diplomatic cost.
- Useful origin relationships.
- A good fit with the existing harem.
- An emergent history with Michael.

## 8.2 Waiting for a better woman

A harem slot may remain empty while the player looks for a better candidate.

Waiting has benefits:

- A more powerful hero may become available.
- Higher-level enemy buildings may generate stronger units.
- A desired notable may become recruitable after the strategic situation changes.
- The player avoids committing a scarce slot prematurely.

Waiting also has costs:

- The party remains smaller and weaker.
- Michael has fewer investigators and field capabilities.
- The desired woman may die.
- She may be moved, promoted, imprisoned, or claimed by Cthulhu.
- Her faction may become inaccessible or cease to exist.
- The main scenario becomes more dangerous over time.

There is no guarantee that waiting produces four ideal heroes.

The exact rule for replacing or dismissing a living main-harem member remains unresolved. It should not behave like casually replacing a piece of equipment, but the cost and permanence of the choice require explicit approval.

## 8.3 Party and strategic roles

Main-harem women receive the deepest available character treatment and serve as active investigators and quest drivers. A main companion may contribute simultaneously to:

- Party combat.
- Exploration.
- Investigation.
- Romance.
- Faction leadership.
- Strategic advice.
- A portfolio such as logistics, military command, magic, diplomacy, intelligence, medicine, recruitment, industry, or naval operations.

The women also develop relationships with one another. The harem is a small emotional and political institution inside Michael's larger faction, not four isolated affection meters.

# 9. Romance-content model

There is no special romanceable caste. Adult female characters participate in one continuous potential relationship system, with different levels of authored depth.

## 9.1 Major authored content

Important notables may receive:

- Bespoke romance arcs.
- Personal quest chains.
- Custom conflicts.
- Political consequences.
- Unique party dialogue.
- Relationships with other major characters.
- Custom reactions to faction events.
- Distinct endings.
- Distinct death, undead, and recovery content.

Some major women receive more content than others. Equal content volume is not required.

## 9.2 Generated romance content

Normal generated women may receive lower-cost personal and romance quests assembled from persistent facts such as:

- Occupation.
- Faction.
- Building of origin.
- Family or friends.
- Rivalries.
- Current morale.
- A recent battle.
- A dead or undead acquaintance.
- A desired object.
- A personal ambition.
- Fear of Cthulhu.
- Curiosity about a Michael machine.
- A grievance with her faction.
- A location that matters to her.

Generated quests should be short and contextual. They need not imitate the scope of authored romances, but they must change remembered state such as attraction, trust, loyalty, morale, relationships, recruitment availability, equipment, skill, or later dialogue.

## 9.3 Emergent relationship content

Faction events naturally create romance material:

- Michael rescues a worker from a collapsing building.
- A fighter asks him to recover a dead squadmate before midnight.
- An officer must decide between Michael and troops who depend on her.
- A cultist seeks help resisting madness.
- An undead woman recognizes Michael through Cthulhu's control.
- A recruited woman wants her family protected.
- Two potential companions dislike one another.
- A woman joins for the machines and only later becomes interested in Michael.

The simulation should supply circumstances; authored and procedural writing turns those circumstances into character stories.

# 10. Morale, rebellion, and ordinary faction allegiance

Men and women in the established ordinary factions can rebel when morale and loyalty collapse.

Morale represents current conditions. Loyalty represents the person's deeper attachment to faction, people, leaders, culture, or cause.

Relevant pressures include:

- Food and water.
- Safety.
- Pay or rewards.
- Housing.
- Victory and defeat.
- Casualties.
- Leadership competence.
- Treatment by superiors.
- Supply failures.
- Weather.
- Corruption.
- Resurrection access.
- Whether loved ones are protected or abandoned.
- Whether the faction appears capable of survival.

Rebellion may produce:

- Refusal.
- Desertion.
- Sabotage.
- Mutiny.
- A splinter group.
- A reform movement.
- Independent settlement.
- Release of prisoners.
- Theft of supplies.
- Assistance to an enemy.

Rebellion does not automatically transfer a unit to Michael. A female rebel remains potentially recruitable by Michael, while a male rebel remains outside Michael's recruitment ability.

Ordinary factions may accept refugees, negotiate personnel transfers, or work with rebels, but they do not possess an active power that takes loyal enemy units. Michael and Cthulhu alone have that strategic privilege.

# 11. Death and the midnight resurrection rule

Every mortal character can die.

Death creates a persistent interval rather than immediately erasing the character:

> alive -> dead and recoverable -> restored by a faction or ally, or automatically raised for Cthulhu at midnight

A dead character retains:

- Identity.
- Name.
- Faction at death.
- Relationships.
- Skills.
- Hero status.
- Equipment where recoverable.
- Death location.
- Cause and time of death.
- Recovery and restoration state.

## 11.1 Before midnight

The character's faction or an allied faction may attempt to recover and restore the dead person before midnight using machinery, ritual, magic, medicine, or another faction-appropriate system.

The accepted rule is that a successful friendly restoration returns the character to the faction held at death. An allied faction performing the restoration gains trust, leverage, payment, or political benefit rather than ownership of the person.

Exact restoration cost, facility throughput, body-recovery requirements, and the treatment of deaths immediately before midnight remain open. Limited capacity is a strong provisional direction because unlimited resurrection would erase the strategic meaning of death.

## 11.2 At midnight

Every dead character not successfully restored is automatically raised on Cthulhu's side.

This is the global resurrection that favors Cthulhu. Friendly restoration and the automatic midnight resurrection are different processes.

The raised character preserves enough identity to remain consequential. A normal worker becomes a modest undead unit. A hero may return with powerful corrupted abilities. A dead main-harem companion becomes a named personal enemy and recovery objective rather than disappearing.

Exact rules for repeated death of an already-undead unit remain unresolved.

## 11.3 Michael's dead women

A woman who joined Michael can die and rise for Cthulhu. Her living allegiance did not fail; death created the transfer.

Even as a Cthulhu unit, she continues to love Michael. That surviving bond may cause recognition, hesitation, resistance, communication, or other story behavior, but it does not automatically teleport her back or make her harmless.

Michael must make an effort to reacquire her. He may need to:

- Locate her.
- Reach her.
- Defeat or separate surrounding Cthulhu units.
- Trigger recognition.
- Break a command tether.
- Use an artifact.
- Restore memory.
- Capture her without destroying her.
- Bring her to machinery or ritual.
- Convince her to return while she remains a sapient undead woman.

Because her love and allegiance already existed, reacquiring her is substantially easier than recruiting an unfamiliar Cthulhu woman.

She may return through either of two broad routes:

- Restoration to life, followed by immediate return to Michael's faction.
- Release from Cthulhu's control while remaining a sapient undead woman.

The second route permits an undead woman to remain a main-harem member if she retains or recovers her identity and self-direction.

## 11.4 Other factions reclaiming their people

Other factions can recover people taken by Cthulhu even though they cannot generally steal enemy units.

They may use faction-appropriate:

- Resurrection machinery.
- Rituals.
- Purification.
- Ancestral or spiritual magic.
- Medical treatment.
- Psychological treatment.
- Artifacts.
- Destruction of Cthulhu control sites.
- Recovery of memories, names, or personal objects.

The restored unit returns to its pre-Cthulhu faction. If that faction has been eliminated, the character's resulting allegiance remains an open rule and should be resolved through surviving relationships and circumstances rather than automatic ownership by the restorer.

# 12. Cthulhu's two recruitment systems

Cthulhu gains units through both living madness and dead resurrection.

## 12.1 Madness recruitment

Cthulhu can recruit living men and women through madness.

Madness may grow through:

- Corrupted terrain.
- Magical weather.
- Nightmares.
- Psychic attacks.
- Cursed artifacts.
- Ritual sites.
- Isolation.
- Despair.
- Faction collapse.
- Repeated exposure to undead acquaintances.
- Corrupted food or water.
- Cthulhu heroes and buildings.
- Witnessing unnatural events.

The progression may be gradual:

1. Fear, insomnia, obsession, unreliable perception, and morale damage.
2. Misrouted supplies, false reports, gate opening, sabotage, cult organization, or protection of corruption.
3. Living allegiance transfer to Cthulhu.

Cthulhu can take men and women. That gives it broader living recruitment coverage than Michael.

Women taken by madness remain potentially recruitable by Michael. Women already belonging to Michael may suffer madness symptoms but do not complete the living faction-transfer step; death remains Cthulhu's route to acquiring them.

## 12.2 Midnight recruitment

Every unresolved death supplies Cthulhu with an undead unit at midnight, including casualties from wars in which Cthulhu did not participate.

This produces a compounding loop:

> ordinary faction war -> casualties -> failed recovery -> midnight undead -> stronger Cthulhu force -> more madness and casualties

Madness produces living recruits. Midnight produces undead recruits. Each system strengthens the other.

## 12.3 Recruitment-resistant force composition

Cthulhu has no protected female characters. Its relative resistance to Michael comes from the large share of its roster that is outside Michael's target category:

- Male cultists.
- Male corrupted units.
- Male undead.
- Mindless undead masses without an individual female person presently accessible.
- Beasts.
- Reanimated animals.
- Aberrations.
- Summoned entities.
- Environmental manifestations.
- Corrupted structures.
- Non-person monsters.

Its actual female characters remain recruitable by Michael.

## 12.4 Female Cthulhu characters

Cthulhu may contain:

- Living female cultists.
- Living women recruited through madness.
- Madness-corrupted women who remain alive.
- Female hero units.
- Sapient undead women.
- Former Michael women raised at midnight.
- Female characters transformed into monstrous but still individually self-aware forms.

Some madness-corrupted women may remain dangerous, unstable, attractive, and romance-capable without being fully cured. Some sapient undead women may remain undead, attractive, intelligent, and suitable for the main harem after Michael breaks or redirects their allegiance.

A mindless undead body cannot participate in romance while no functioning individual remains. If the original woman can be restored sufficiently to become a character again, she re-enters Michael's recruitment domain. This is restoration of the woman, not an immunity exception.

## 12.5 Starting Cthulhu hero roster

Cthulhu begins with ten authored heroes: five men and five women. These are physical characters in the simulation, not abstract faction modifiers. They travel, fight, command, become injured, form relationships, can be captured or killed, and can return through the midnight system when their bodies remain unresolved.

The five women remain potentially recruitable by Michael. The five men remain outside Michael's special recruitment ability. A recruited woman keeps her identity, party passive, strategic passive, four powers, ultimate, history, and relationships; the complete hero kit changes allegiance with her.

The portraits below are approved as the actual character photographs and production identity anchors.

### Gideon Vale - The High Priest

![Gideon Vale - approved character photograph](assets/characters/gideon-vale.png)

Gideon is the smiling prophet and false savior: an elegant, beautifully spoken cult leader who appears wherever frightened people have been abandoned. He listens, remembers names, distributes food, comforts the grieving, and never needs to raise his voice. His attractiveness and apparent kindness are strategic weapons because surrender genuinely appears merciful when he describes it.

He believes fear, responsibility, grief, and individual choice cause human suffering, and that Cthulhu offers release from all four. He is not lying when he says he wants to save people. The horror is that he means salvation through consumption.

His approved photograph locks the pale preacher's coat, dark teal formal layers, wet cuffs, gloved extended hand, occult book-cane, composed charm, and subtle looming Cthulhu presence. He should be remembered as the villain who was kind to everyone and meant every word.

His complete encounter kit remains open. It must support a readable charismatic high-priest fantasy in direct battle rather than spending power slots on generic morale accounting.

### Admiral Silas Blackwake - The Drowned Warlord

![Admiral Silas Blackwake - approved character photograph](assets/characters/admiral-silas-blackwake.png)

Silas is an undead admiral who still conducts the war that destroyed his fleet. He marches from the sea with dead sailors in disciplined ranks and treats Cthulhu's dead as the fleet he was always meant to command. He cannot admit that his last living command killed his sailors unnecessarily, because his entire identity depends on obedience having given those deaths meaning.

Pirates disgust him as freedom without discipline. Michael fascinates and infuriates him as another captain whose followers may choose to stay. Silas should enter play as a military parade emerging from the surf after midnight.

His approved photograph locks the enormous ceremonial silhouette, drowned officer's uniform, corroded medals, naval saber, anchor chain, seawater, dignified undeath, and commanding stance. He is imposing and intact rather than a decomposing corpse. He should be remembered as the admiral whose drowned army has finally marched ashore.

His complete encounter kit remains open and must make him play as an advancing commander and warlord, not merely as a passive undead-production bonus.

### Doctor Orfeo Venn - The Mad Doctor

![Doctor Orfeo Venn - approved character photograph](assets/characters/doctor-orfeo-venn.png)

Orfeo is a forbidden natural philosopher who no longer distinguishes engineering from medicine. He sees the island as one wounded organism: roads are cuts, fortifications are splints, settlements are infections, and individual bodies are material that ought to be returned to the whole.

He possesses great tenderness and no respect for individual boundaries. He praises an enemy's skeleton while trying to remove it and becomes sincerely offended when anyone calls his creations disgusting. In his view, he is the only person attempting to heal anything.

His approved photograph locks the elegant dark surgical coat, restrained instruments, gloves, biological specimen, glass-and-brass apparatus, saw-cane, intellectual excitement, and attractive human integrity. His creations carry the body horror; his own body does not become a random collection of exposed organs. He should be remembered as the doctor who wants to heal the island by sewing everyone into it.

His complete encounter kit remains open and must make his medical creation fantasy physically playable and visibly counterable.

### Jonah Rook - The Gravekeeper

![Jonah Rook - approved character photograph](assets/characters/jonah-rook.png)

Jonah is the midnight bellman: a huge, solemn undertaker who arrives after battle to identify and collect everyone the living failed to carry home. He once buried the unknown dead after wars and disasters and became unable to tolerate anonymous graves. Cthulhu promised that no body would ever be lost or forgotten again.

He records names, knows who loved the dead, and treats bodies with great care while taking them forever. The player should hear his bell through the fog while racing the midnight deadline long before seeing the man himself.

His approved photograph locks the strong weathered face, broad reassuring build, gravekeeper's coat, keys and burial tags, wooden yoke, and enormous Cthulhu-marked funeral bell. He is frightening through purpose and solidity rather than deformity. He should be remembered as the man who comes to collect everyone the living leave behind.

His complete encounter kit remains open. The bell must become a clear physical combat instrument and not merely a narrative alarm or strategic corpse counter.

### Dagan Brine - The Deep Hunter

![Dagan Brine - approved character photograph](assets/characters/dagan-brine.png)

Dagan is the harpooner below: a relentless drowned hunter who treats every connected body of water as one hunting ground. He can disappear into a flooded cellar, rise from a village well, hook a defender from formation, or wait beneath apparently harmless water.

He survived his crew's sinking by cutting himself free. He has built a theology around denying that choice: the sea takes everyone, nobody survives because they deserve to, and nobody is responsible for whom they abandon. Cthulhu lets him make that claim true by pulling the dry world into the sea.

His approved photograph locks the rugged intact sailor, powerful working build, wet black hunting gear, rope, chain, scars, forward stance, and signature barbed harpoon. He is physically dangerous rather than grotesque. He should be remembered as the hunter who can wait in any water deep enough to hide a hand.

His complete encounter kit remains open and must revolve around hunting, hooking, water access, pursuit, and visible counterplay.

### Mirelle Voss - The Oracle

![Mirelle Voss - approved character photograph](assets/characters/mirelle-voss.png)

Mirelle is the impossible oracle: a woman who remembers events that have not happened and cannot determine which of her desires are truly her own. She speaks specifically rather than in decorative riddles. She remembers arguments Michael has not yet had, apologizes for wounds nobody has received, and grieves outcomes before a conversation begins.

What she wants is not simply sanity. She wants to make one genuine choice that no god, prophecy, or future self has already placed before her. That gives Michael a meaningful recruitment and romance problem: attraction is easy compared with proving that her decision to join him is actually hers.

Her approved photograph locks the beautiful adult face, very slim form, teal-and-violet ritual clothing, veil and net elements, moving prophecy marks across the bare abdomen, mirrored or ritual knife, intimate gaze, and uncanny composure. She is attractive and vulnerable without becoming physically broken or repulsive.

Her complete encounter kit remains open. It must turn impossible foresight into concrete, readable encounter actions and counterplay rather than making her merely a woman who knows the weather or supplies strategic forecasts.

### Lady Isolde Marr - The Dark Duelist

![Lady Isolde Marr - approved character photograph](assets/characters/lady-isolde-marr.png)

Isolde is the dead countess: a beautiful undead aristocrat who has retained her intelligence, vanity, appetite, and perfect confidence with a blade. Undeath has removed the stakes from what she once loved. Victory means little if she expects to stand again at midnight, so she searches for an opponent capable of making danger feel real.

Michael interests her because he is inventive, reckless, mortal, and building something that may outlast him. Her story does not need to begin with curing her. It begins when he treats her undeath as a fact rather than the entirety of her identity.

Her approved photograph locks the lithe fencing build, aristocratic cropped dueling jacket, fitted dark trousers, tall boots, bare waist, rapier, pistol, poised challenge, and elegant dark-red-and-black palette. Her undeath must remain alluring and legible in future production rather than turning her into a rotten enemy version and a beautiful recruited version.

Her complete encounter kit remains open and must deliver a fast, formal, technically brilliant duelist with visible challenges and counters.

### Nerissa Gale - The Sea Witch

![Nerissa Gale - approved character photograph](assets/characters/nerissa-gale.png)

Nerissa is a storm-calling sea witch, not a weather analyst. Before Cthulhu, she correctly predicted a catastrophic storm and was ignored. The ship sailed and many people died. Now the storm listens, and every bolt of lightning is an argument she can finally win.

She wants obedience rather than acknowledgment because subtle warnings once saved nobody. Michael is one of the few men capable of building instruments that prove what she senses and one of the few arrogant enough to stand in her storm and argue back.

Her approved photograph locks the slim athletic adult silhouette, wild wet hair, pointed ears, cropped dark sea-witch clothing, net construction, bare midriff, strong legs, water spiral, lightning gesture, and commanding upward gaze. She looks like the sea ignored the dress code.

Her complete encounter kit remains open and must make her physically call and ride a storm through visible battlefield actions rather than allocating powers to forecasts or weather administration.

### Sabine Coil - The Beast Mistress

![Sabine Coil - approved character photograph](assets/characters/sabine-coil.png)

Sabine is the mother of monsters: a wild, magnetic keeper who loves creatures more easily than people. She names every abomination, praises it when it tears through fortifications, scolds soldiers who frighten it, and becomes furious when commanders waste living creatures in suicidal attacks.

She believes monstrosity is life unconstrained by human vanity. Cthulhu gives her access to impossible creatures but treats them as disposable growths, creating the fault line in her loyalty. Michael's mechanical animals initially offend her as hollow copies; their accumulated quirks, repairs, and relationships may eventually convince her to name them too.

Her approved photograph locks the lean athletic adult build, fitted creature-handler leathers, exposed midriff, netted sleeves and stockings, low utility belt, practical boots, confident warmth, and especially the affectionate shoulder relationship with her many-legged companion. She should look like a woman who can soothe a monster and ride it into battle.

Her complete encounter kit remains open and must center on distinct named creatures and direct handler behavior rather than a generic pet-class package.

### Evelyn Mourne - The Dollmaker

![Evelyn Mourne - approved character photograph](assets/characters/evelyn-mourne.png)

Evelyn is the first hero concept in this pass whose complete game design has reached a stable center. She is a ranged summoner and battlefield controller whose four powers each summon one distinct doll and whose ultimate assembles her masterwork doll.

Evelyn was a celebrated artisan who built dolls to embody a human quality perfectly. People contradict themselves, become afraid after promising courage, withdraw affection, change loyalty, and die before completing the roles she imagined for them. A soldier doll remains brave. A nurse remains kind. A dancer never refuses. A scapegoat accepts suffering without accusation.

Cthulhu gave the dolls motion, appetite, memory, and fragments of voices. Evelyn's central contradiction is that she desperately wants companionship but only feels safe loving companions she can completely control. Her relationship with Michael asks whether she can love a real person precisely because he can surprise, contradict, refuse, disappoint, and leave her.

Her approved photograph locks the beautiful adult gothic face, slim form, exposed belly, fitted black-and-crimson costume, open thread or net construction, low tool belts, high boots, cabinet silhouette, puppeteering hands, red and black threads, and four visually distinct dolls. The dolls carry the overt horror. Evelyn remains attractive, composed, and intimate.

## 12.6 Evelyn Mourne's accepted hero kit

### Role and loop

**Role:** Ranged summoner and battlefield controller.

**Loop:** Summon the household, position each doll, issue each special command, keep the collection alive, and combine the surviving collection into The Favorite.

Evelyn is personally fragile. She becomes powerful only after gaining enough time and space to establish her household. Her dolls are hero summons and do not count as additional Cthulhu base-unit types.

### Basic attack - Needlework

Evelyn fires enchanted sewing needles from a compact wrist mechanism. A supernatural thread briefly connects each needle to her. Needlework lets her defend herself before the collection is assembled, finish badly wounded targets, and direct the dolls toward her current priority. The dolls, not the basic attack, remain her main source of power.

### Party passive - Little Hands Everywhere

While any doll is active, physical dolls can perform nearby interactions for the group: pull small levers, crawl through narrow openings, retrieve keys or dropped equipment, hold pressure plates, check the far side of a door, begin stabilizing a downed character, pull a small object or wounded person toward safety, or stand watch during rest. A doll must actually reach the interaction. Destruction, confinement, or failed pathing causes the attempt to fail.

### Strategic passive - The Dollhouse Network

With access to a suitable workshop, ritual room, or quarters, Evelyn can create a limited number of sentinel dolls for faction buildings. Each assigned doll observes the interior, warns of unauthorized entry, notices obvious sabotage, identifies who moved an object or opened a gate, and acts as a small alarm carrier or defender.

Sentinels are physical and counterable. An infiltrator can notice, blind, trap, destroy, substitute, carry away, or deliberately mislead one. If Evelyn changes faction, distant sentinels do not change allegiance automatically; she must reclaim, abandon, destroy, or replace them. This can leave behind espionage and recovery quests.

### Power one - The Soldier

Evelyn deploys a squat painted-wood soldier in tin armor carrying a disproportionately heavy shield. It guards Evelyn, blocks narrow approaches, intercepts attacks, protects an injured ally when Evelyn is safe, and uses a tiny bayonet against anything passing its shield.

Recasting orders **March**: the Soldier braces its shield, advances in a straight line, pushes aside small enemies, interrupts the first substantial target, and establishes a temporary protected position. Large enemies stop it. Flanking, lifting it before it braces, armor breaking, sustained area damage, and forced separation are its principal counters.

### Power two - The Nurse

Evelyn deploys a pale porcelain nurse that unfolds to approximately child-doll scale. It stitches ordinary wounds, stops bleeding, stabilizes downed characters, repairs cloth dolls, replaces thread, and mends cracked porcelain. It does not resurrect; it protects the interval in which recovery or resurrection remains possible.

Recasting orders **Emergency Stitch**: the Nurse rushes to one ally and performs a concentrated intervention, stopping active bleeding, removing one physical wound complication, restoring a damaged doll, stabilizing a critical character, or restraining an injury that would otherwise deteriorate. The effort leaves the Nurse cracked and vulnerable. Crushing, fire, displacement, contamination, and concentrated damage counter it.

### Power three - The Scapegoat

Evelyn constructs a pinned humanoid rag effigy in the likeness of a selected enemy. She requires a valid connection such as a name, blood, hair, torn clothing, reflection, or thread placed by Needlework. Attacking the effigy transfers a controlled portion of harm to the target; binding or pinning parts of the doll imposes corresponding hindrances.

Recasting orders **Put a Pin in It**, selecting one temporary effect: hand disrupts weapon use, foot slows movement, mouth interrupts a command or spell, and heart increases transferred damage. A visible thread identifies the link. Range, barriers, cleansing, destruction of the doll, displacement of Evelyn, or removal of the establishing sample provide counterplay.

The Scapegoat is a humanoid rag effigy. It is not a literal goat.

### Power four - The Dancer

Evelyn releases a tall narrow wooden marionette with elongated articulated limbs. It circles priority targets, harasses anyone approaching Evelyn, entangles exposed weapons, punishes stationary enemies, and lays visible strings across important paths.

Recasting orders **Dance With Me**: the Dancer attaches strings to a target and attempts a short forced dance, pulling ordinary enemies several steps, turning them, moving them out of cover, or interrupting a prepared attack. Heroes and large monsters resist full control but may be slowed, partially turned, or interrupted while the strings remain intact. Cutting the strings, destroying the fragile marionette, blocking it with terrain, unstoppable movement, hazards, or pressure on Evelyn provide counterplay.

### Ultimate - The Favorite

Evelyn opens the largest cabinet compartment and reveals a beautiful, human-sized masterwork assembled from porcelain, cloth, wood, metal joints, hair, and pieces of the active household. Each living normal doll incorporated at the moment of summoning grants one faculty:

- The Soldier grants armor, interception, and shield behavior.
- The Nurse grants regeneration, stabilization, and self-repair.
- The Scapegoat grants a damage link to the selected enemy.
- The Dancer grants speed, long thread attacks, and forced movement.

The Favorite can be assembled early from fewer dolls or delayed for the complete combination. A player may also intentionally preserve one doll outside the ultimate to retain its independent function. When The Favorite ends, incorporated dolls fall inert and must be repaired or resummoned.

Ultimate charge comes from each doll successfully performing its defining role: meaningful interception, prevented death or restoration, transferred harm or crucial interruption, and consequential displacement. The player earns the masterwork by using the whole collection well rather than waiting for a timer.

### Controls, AI, strengths, and counters

The dolls use competent defaults and do not demand miniature RTS micromanagement. Each doll button summons the doll when absent and issues its special command when present. A shared command recalls the collection.

Enemy or allied AI uses the same kit. Evelyn prioritizes immediate defense, sustained recovery, linking the most dangerous accessible enemy, controlling mobile or protected targets, avoiding telegraphed area attacks, assembling The Favorite from at least three useful dolls when possible, and retreating when the cabinet is disabled.

Evelyn is strong in prolonged encounters, protection, positioning, indirect attacks, stabilization, and environmental interaction. She is weak when rushed, silenced, separated from the dolls, exposed to area damage, denied valid links, or forced to replace summons reactively.

If recruited, she remains the Dollmaker. Michael may improve joints, springs, armor, signals, needle launchers, fire resistance, or replaceable parts, but these are progression and equipment. They do not replace her supernatural hero identity.

## 12.7 Hero-sheet production contract

Every authored hero must eventually contain:

1. Name.
2. Character concept and narrative.
3. Party passive.
4. Strategic passive.
5. Four deliberate encounter powers.
6. One visible encounter ultimate.
7. Basic attack or repeatable physical action.
8. Movement style.
9. Encounter loop and resource, stance, setup, or signature state.
10. Clear party reason.
11. Visible counterplay.
12. Recruitment, alliance, capture, defeat, death, and resurrection conditions where applicable.

The four powers describe what the character physically does during playable encounters. The party passive describes what changes naturally in the local group. The strategic passive describes what the owning faction can perceive, decide, preserve, exploit, or recover because it possesses the hero.

Strategic passives belong to the character. They transfer with allegiance and stop when death, capture, isolation, or loss of communication removes the hero from strategic participation. They must create a noticeable hole when lost and must not degrade into an anonymous percentage bonus.

Hero AI uses the same powers and counterplay as the player-facing version. Ultimates charge through actions expressing the hero's concept rather than generic elapsed time.

The current ten Cthulhu identities and photographs are accepted. Evelyn's complete kit is accepted at the system level, with exact numbers left tunable. The other nine Cthulhu kits and Captain Michael's kit remain open and must be derived from their accepted character centers.

# 13. Cthulhu's main-scenario forcing function

At neutral baseline, factions should be designed to compete without requiring scenario cheats to cover structural weakness. The main campaign then deliberately gives Cthulhu an advantage that grows over time.

The advantage arises through several connected systems:

- Madness recruitment of living men and women.
- Automatic midnight recruitment of unresolved dead.
- Corruption of terrain.
- Magical weather increasingly favorable to Cthulhu.
- State-gated authored events that reinforce visible Cthulhu momentum.
- The ability to exploit wars, rebellions, damaged infrastructure, and overloaded resurrection systems.
- Progress toward the eventual summoning of the major Cthulhu threat.

The campaign begins as a factional slugfest. Ordinary factions initially have rational reasons to treat one another as immediate enemies. Their wars then generate bodies, instability, and madness that increasingly strengthen Cthulhu.

## 13.1 Actual and perceived threat

The simulation distinguishes:

- **Actual Cthulhu pressure:** the hidden scenario state determining its growing advantages and eventual confrontation.
- **Perceived Cthulhu threat:** what each faction currently believes based on evidence it has received.

Faction AI must not behave as though it has read the scenario design. It learns through scouts, casualties, returned dead, corrupted terrain, refugees, allies, weather, missing units, failed routes, named characters, and direct attacks.

Recognition should develop roughly as:

> rumor -> local danger -> regional threat -> strategic priority -> existential enemy

Alliances should likewise emerge through:

> shared concern -> information exchange -> ceasefire -> joint defense -> strategic coalition

There is no required universal alliance switch on a predetermined day. Hatred, ambition, ignorance, denial, and opportunity may delay cooperation. Some factions may try to exploit the crisis rather than unite.

The previously accepted Day 100 and hidden-pressure confrontation concepts remain part of the wider scenario authority, but their exact interaction with the new madness and midnight systems requires a later consolidation pass.

# 14. Alliances and strategic decisions

Factions can form, revise, and break alliances.

Possible agreements include:

- Non-aggression.
- Trade.
- Safe passage.
- Shared roads or ports.
- Intelligence exchange.
- Corpse recovery access.
- Use of allied resurrection infrastructure.
- Prisoner exchange.
- Joint attack.
- Mutual defense.
- Territorial guarantees.
- Anti-Cthulhu coalition.

Faction decisions consider:

- Survival.
- Territory.
- Population.
- Morale.
- Rebellion risk.
- Resources.
- Supply and routes.
- Building levels.
- Military strength.
- Enemy strength.
- Opportunities.
- Grievances.
- Trust.
- Leader relationships.
- Treaty history.
- Shared threats.
- Actual observed Cthulhu activity.
- Resurrection access.
- The danger of producing more undead through continued war.

An alliance does not erase conflict. Two factions may cooperate against Cthulhu while disputing territory or withholding information. A faction may rescue an ally's dead because it needs that ally to survive. Another may allow a rival's dead to rise because it expects Cthulhu to attack elsewhere, only to suffer the consequences later.

Michael's recruitment can improve or damage alliances. A woman may become a valuable diplomatic bridge, or her departure may become a major grievance. The reaction depends on her value and the circumstances rather than a universal penalty.

# 15. Distinct faction battlefield niches

Each faction must be recognizable through what it does in battle, not merely through costume or architecture.

A complete faction requires:

- A distinct economy.
- Distinct building cycles.
- Distinct population and reinforcement sources.
- A clear tactical verb.
- Unique movement behavior.
- Unique formation or spacing.
- Unique range and attack cadence.
- Unique terrain interaction.
- Unique pets, creatures, constructs, or summons where appropriate.
- Distinct hero roles.
- Distinct strengths and counterplay.
- Distinct dungeon hazards and loot.
- Distinct AI preferences.
- A victory doctrine.
- A recovery doctrine.

At gameplay distance, the player should recognize the faction from silhouette, movement, formation, attacks, terrain effects, reinforcement method, and reaction to casualties.

## 15.1 Accepted and provisional niche directions

Captain Michael's niche and Cthulhu's broad niche are established. The remaining four directions are useful provisional anchors, not complete approved faction designs.

| Faction | Battlefield verb | Current directional identity |

# 18. Balance implications

The faction system is intentionally asymmetric.

## 18.1 Michael

Michael begins with almost nothing but can eventually combine the best female personnel from every faction with the strongest technology on the island. He has narrow organic recruitment coverage but exceptional permanence: women who join him do not defect while alive.

His main risks are the solo opening, industrial disruption, organic scarcity, political backlash, and death before resurrection.

## 18.2 Cthulhu

Cthulhu can take living men and women through madness and receives all unresolved dead at midnight. Its army also contains many male, monstrous, undead, summoned, and environmental units outside Michael's recruitment domain.

Its main-scenario strength grows from time, war, death, madness, weather, corruption, and weighted authored events. Exact baseline weaknesses and counterplay must be completed in the dedicated Cthulhu faction pass.

## 18.3 Ordinary factions

The four ordinary factions do not need unit-stealing powers. Their complete designs must give them equally strong but qualitatively different ways to survive, grow, fight, recover, ally, and pursue victory.

Their ordinary buildings continually create units. Their heroes matter. Their pets or other special unit classes can insulate parts of their roster from Michael. Their recovery systems can reclaim members from Cthulhu. Their battlefield niches must remain obvious and powerful.

## 18.4 Main-scenario imbalance is deliberate

Neutral faction balance permits sandboxes and alternate scenarios. The main campaign then applies a deliberate Cthulhu forcing function. Weather and scripted bonuses amplify an already functioning faction rather than concealing a weak design.

## 18.5 Global character-art direction

Pirate Island is simultaneously an autonomous faction RTS, a party RPG, and a romance game. Important characters must serve all three layers.

The global casting rule is:

> The people are desirable. The things happening to them, around them, and because of them may be horrific.

Every adult woman is deliberately designed to be attractive. Every named adult man has a compelling form of attractiveness through charm, competence, authority, intelligence, danger, reliability, style, physical capability, or weathered humanity. Important characters do not become repulsive merely because they serve Cthulhu.

Grotesque horror belongs principally to monsters, failed resurrection, summoned things, transformations, environmental corruption, flesh growths, reanimated animals, parasitic structures, impossible movement, and what attractive characters do to other people.

### Female house style

The default female direction is:

- Unmistakably adult.
- Between approximately 4 feet 10 inches and 5 feet 3 inches in diegetic height.
- Never taller than 5 feet 3 inches without a later explicit exception.
- Slim, lithe, and lightly built.
- Small-busted or flat-chested by default.
- Cute-to-beautiful face, with the face and expression as the first visual priority.
- Clean shoulder, waist, belly, hip, leg, and posture lines.
- Hips from subtle to moderately present.
- A perky, proportionate, noticeable rear silhouette without exaggeration.
- Deliberately sexy PG-13 clothing rather than nudity or breast-first fantasy anatomy.
- A distinct appeal through elegance, danger, wildness, competence, gothic intimacy, cuteness, or ethereal beauty.

Attraction should be carried by the complete design: face, expression, waist, exposed belly where suitable, hips, legs, posture, motion, clothing construction, confidence, vulnerability, danger, humor, competence, and personal attention. Breast size must not become the default maturity signal or central composition.

Large breasts, extreme cleavage, top-heavy anatomy, balloon-like hips or buttocks, implausibly tiny waists, child-coded proportions, and generic fantasy pinup anatomy are art-direction failures unless a specific exception is deliberately approved.

Short stature must never be communicated through child coding. Adult facial structure, adult torso and limb proportions, adult posture, mature costume construction, voice, behavior, and social role remain mandatory throughout the 4-foot-10-inch to 5-foot-3-inch range. The height range describes scale, not age.

The height difference between women and men is an intentional part of the game's visual and romantic composition. Party lineups, dialogue staging, embraces, confrontations, combat poses, shared machinery, and hero silhouettes should use the contrast rather than normalizing everyone toward one interchangeable heroic height. The contrast should make the women look compact, adult, agile, and individually striking while allowing male characters to carry a visibly different physical presence. Exact male heights remain character-specific and unresolved; no universal male range is invented here.

### Rebellious PG-13 costume language

The internal costume target is knowingly one step beyond respectable while remaining non-nude and non-parodic. A woman should look as though she chose the outfit, knows it pushes social limits, and looks excellent wearing it.

Useful devices include cropped coats and tops, visible midriffs, belly windows, open backs or shoulders, fitted trousers or shorts, split skirts, high slits, tall boots, stockings, low-slung belts, selective straps, mesh, lace, netting, and open stitching. Each outfit should choose one principal exposure idea and one or two supports instead of displaying every device simultaneously.

Netting must inherit the character and faction: sea knots for Nerissa, black doll thread for Evelyn, handler rigging for Sabine, formal rebellious lace for a colonial woman, magical fiber for an elf, decorative lattice for a fox woman, or engineered flexible mesh for a Michael recruit.

No nudity, visible nipples or genitals, explicitly transparent intimate coverage, explicit sexual action, accidental-wardrobe-malfunction design, or child-coded presentation is allowed. Rule of cool outranks museum-level historical accuracy, but costumes must remain coherent with the alternate nineteenth-century fantasy and the character's movement.

### Male house style

Adult male characters range from approximately 5 feet 9 inches to 6 feet 4 inches. All therefore read as tall beside the approved female range without becoming overwhelmingly giant. Nimble, quick, precise, or strongly cerebral men generally occupy the lower end. Increasingly physical bruisers occupy progressively taller and heavier points toward the upper end according to how much of their identity depends on force.

Named men may be lean, athletic, broad, heavy, old, young, scarred, weathered, elegant, severe, undead, or strange. They should retain charisma, dignity, an expressive face, useful-looking hands, a strong readable silhouette, and a credible form of appeal. They should not become faceless deformities, bloated corpse caricatures, incoherent exposed-organ assemblages, or interchangeable villain brutes.

The upper end is reserved for genuine bruiser silhouettes rather than used as a universal heroic default. A 6-foot-4-inch bruiser may dominate a doorway and melee composition, but that size narrows the character's role and imposes appropriate limits through some combination of slower repositioning, larger navigation clearance, easier targeting, reduced access to cramped paths, conspicuous presence, or reliance on direct physical engagement. Exact limitations belong to each completed kit; height is not a free universal combat bonus.

### Production admission test

Every final character asset must pass three views:

1. **Portrait attraction:** face and expression make the player want to know the person.
2. **Full-body attraction:** figure, costume, posture, and signature prop support the intended appeal.
3. **Gameplay recognition:** silhouette, weapon, outer shape, faction read, and motion remain legible from the fixed isometric camera.

The approved character photographs are portrait and full-body identity anchors. They do not prove that an eventual isometric model passes the third test.

The standalone portraits do not establish absolute character height because they contain no common scale reference. Character model sheets and party lineups must add a shared measurement grid. Isometric models, navigation capsules, interaction anchors, camera framing, animation retargeting, and paired-character scenes must enforce the approved female height range consistently.

The same shared measurement grid must enforce the 5-foot-9-inch to 6-foot-4-inch male range and preserve the intentional inter-sex height contrast. Doorways, cover, vehicles, mechanical mounts, paired animations, weapon reach, and camera framing must be tested against both extremes: a 4-foot-10-inch woman and a 6-foot-4-inch male bruiser.

## 18.6 Character-photo continuity and provenance

The approved photographs are stored in `outputs/assets/characters/`. Their hashes, source-generation identifiers, approval state, transfer requirements, and rejected predecessors are recorded in `outputs/assets/characters/character-art-manifest.json`.

# 19. Provisional ordinary-faction comparative plan

**Status:** Provisional faction-chassis plan. The comparative population premise is accepted; exact rosters, building titles, resource costs, spawn timings, hero identities, and numerical balance remain subject to faction-by-faction approval.

## 19.1 The six-faction population balance

The six factions answer population gain, allegiance transfer, and permanent loss through different systems.

- Michael can recruit any adult woman under achievable conditions. Every success removes a real worker, fighter, specialist, notable, leader, or hero from the source faction.
- Cthulhu can convert living men and women through madness and gains every unresolved dead character at midnight.
- Pirates generate a modest native population and offer gold, shares, escape, and opportunity to dissatisfied living men and women. Michael-aligned women are categorically unavailable because their living allegiance does not break.
- Fox people generate population through distributed hearths, while individual fox women can woo living men into their households and faction through personal relationships.
- Colonial powers possess the strongest straightforward population throughput through ports, settlements, housing, institutions, and scheduled arrivals.
- Elves begin with a small population and generate new elves only through an exceptionally slow, tightly capped sanctuary cycle. They can recover from permanent losses over long spans, but never fast enough to trade units efficiently or refill after a disastrous battle.

This asymmetry is intentional. Colonials provide raw population pressure. Fox people combine distributed native growth with male relationship recruitment. Pirates convert gold and dissatisfaction into conditional contract loyalty while stealing movable capability. Michael creates a permanent female following around himself. Cthulhu consumes psychological and physical failure. Elves wager exceptional magic, craft, and hardiness against a replacement cycle too slow to rescue them from serious attrition.

| Faction | Native or created population | Repeatable acquisition from another faction | Acquisition basis | Loyalty character |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Captain Michael | Machines; no organic opening generation | Adult women | Personal attraction, relationship, achievable individual conditions | Permanent while living after willing recruitment |
| Cthulhu | Monsters, summons, corrupted production | Living men and women statistically; unresolved dead deterministically | Madness pressure and the midnight death rule | Supernatural control, corruption, and undeath |
| Pirates | Modest dockside and shipboard generation | Dissatisfied living men and women, excluding Michael-aligned women | Gold, shares, escape, opportunity, and a credible contract | Conditional on pay, shares, leadership, freedom, and continued satisfaction |
| Fox people | Distributed hearth generation | Living men | Individual fox women woo particular men through relationships | Personal and relationship-bound rather than globally permanent |
| Colonial powers | Highest straightforward generation through settlement and institutions | No unique repeatable acquisition power | General diplomacy, immigration, and world events only | Institutional, civic, military, familial, and economic |
| Elves | Extremely slow, capped sanctuary generation | No unique repeatable acquisition power | General diplomacy and world events only | Cultural, personal, ecological, and historical |

The population model for an ordinary faction is conceptually:

```text
current people
+ faction-permitted native generation
+ voluntary immigration or return
+ faction-specific voluntary recruitment
+ successful resurrection or recovery
- combat deaths
- midnight losses
- madness transfers to Cthulhu
- women recruited by Michael
- men wooed into the fox faction
- dissatisfied people hired by pirates
- rebellion, desertion, and negotiated departure
= next population state
```

Every unit retains identity, training, relationships, morale, and history. Replacing headcount never immediately replaces a lost veteran, specialist, hero, commander, family connection, or diplomatic bridge. Elves may eventually replace raw headcount, but their cycle is so slow that every permanent loss remains strategically severe.

Population output is bounded by housing, provisions, safe territory, functioning buildings, morale, and the faction's particular growth system. A faction cannot respond to losses by producing unlimited people from one surviving structure. Elven output is never zero forever, but it remains deliberately too slow and capacity-limited to function as an emergency recharge.

## 19.2 Shared ordinary-faction contracts

The four ordinary factions share these boundaries:

- They do not possess Michael-style permanent female recruitment or Cthulhu-style madness and death conversion.
- They may accept willing refugees, rebels, surrendered characters, negotiated transfers, and returning members.
- Colonial, fox, pirate, and elven buildings generate adult men and women as persistent units according to different rules. Elven generation is exceptionally slow and limited.
- Women remain recruitable by Michael; high population output changes the strategic cost but never creates immunity.
- Men and women remain vulnerable to Cthulhu madness and midnight resurrection.
- Pirates alone use gold and dissatisfaction as a repeatable paid-defection system.
- Fox women alone use distributed wooing of men as a repeatable relationship-recruitment system.
- Each starts with five male and five female heroes.
- Each needs a faction-appropriate body-recovery and resurrection chain.
- Each requires a distinct economy, building cycle, movement doctrine, battlefield verb, dungeon grammar, and victory behavior.
- Each must remain capable of winning a neutral sandbox without scripted rescue.
- Each must recognize Cthulhu gradually from evidence rather than beginning with omniscient anti-Cthulhu coordination.

These shared boundaries are simulation interfaces, not a common play template. There is no universal barracks progression, standard worker-fighter-healer roster, common capture rhythm, or default answer to attack, defense, travel, recovery, and population loss.

The nine ordinary unit families below are internal faction families rather than slots aligned across factions. A colonial line infantry unit is not the colonial skin for an elven sentinel; a fox veil caster is not a recolored elven spell warden; a pirate rigger is not a faster colonial sapper. Similar real-world needs may exist, but their inputs, behaviors, combinations, strengths, failure states, and player decisions must remain different.

### Complete asymmetric coolness contract

Complete mechanical difference is necessary but insufficient. Every faction must also be independently desirable to play, interesting to fight, valuable to ally with, painful to lose, and exciting to encounter at the height of its power. Balance means an equal **fantasy budget** and credible neutral-campaign viability. It does not mean equal tools, matching counters, parallel tech trees, identical attention demands, or mirrored statistics.

Every faction must own all of the following:

1. **An exclusive power fantasy:** something the faction can become or accomplish that no other faction can reproduce by purchasing an equivalent upgrade.
2. **A signature board transformation:** its success makes the island visibly and mechanically different even when faction colors and labels are hidden.
3. **A signature battle story:** players can describe what happened in a battle using events and verbs that identify the faction without naming it.
4. **An escalating spectacle:** growth progresses from a vulnerable local expression to a board-changing apex rather than merely increasing damage and health.
5. **A mastery surface:** experienced direction reveals combinations, timing, positioning, or deception that the autonomous AI can use competently but a skilled player can exploit brilliantly.
6. **A dangerous temptation:** its strongest strategy invites a characteristic risk instead of being the safe answer in every situation.
7. **A catastrophic weakness:** enemies can attack the faction's actual engine and cause a collapse that looks and feels unique to it.
8. **A distinct comeback story:** recovery restores the faction through its own fantasy rather than through a generic replacement army or emergency resource grant.
9. **A campaign-ending expression:** if it dominates the island, the resulting world and player problem are specific to that faction.
10. **A desirable relationship to the RPG layer:** its heroes, recruits, dungeons, alliances, equipment, romance possibilities, and betrayals offer experiences unavailable from the other five factions.

No faction receives a generic unit or universal technology merely to erase its intended weakness. It compensates through its own systems, suitable heroes, diplomacy, terrain, timing, or cooperation. Hard counters should attack the faction's engine and force a different decision; they should not simply turn a battle into an automatic loss.

The provisional apex expressions are deliberately different: a colonial network becomes a fortified state capable of coordinating entire fronts; fox influence turns the enemy's understanding of the island against itself; pirate success produces a roaming flotilla that can make a coastline suddenly unsafe; an elven network makes the terrain itself participate in war; Michael fields extraordinary combined machinery operated by a small, personally assembled society; and Cthulhu converts weather, madness, corruption, and accumulated death into an approaching supernatural catastrophe. These are spectacle directions, not permission to flatten their underlying economies into six ultimate-unit timers.

### Complete-difference admission gate

A faction is not ready for approval unless it passes all of these tests:

1. **Silhouette-blind recognition:** with names, colors, and art removed, an observer can identify the faction from production behavior, movement, orders, and battlefield rhythm.
2. **Different first question:** each faction asks the player or AI a different question at the beginning of a strategic decision. Colonials ask what can be held; fox people ask what the enemy can be made to believe; pirates ask what can be taken before the response arrives; elves ask how the room and region can be reshaped; Michael asks what machinery and infrastructure can multiply scarce people; Cthulhu asks what fear, death, corruption, or time can be converted into power.
3. **Different map ownership:** occupation, distributed social presence, predatory access, ecological connection, industrial linkage, and corruption are different territorial states rather than six colors on the same node flag.
4. **Different force creation:** scheduled colonial settlement, distributed fox hearth growth plus women wooing men, modest pirate households plus paid defection and flexible billets, extremely slow elven sanctuary generation plus intensive cultivation of existing units, Michael's machines plus permanent female recruitment, and Cthulhu's madness plus midnight resurrection are not interchangeable spawn timers.
5. **Different combat rhythm:** formation and preparation, deception and dispersal, rapid concentration and withdrawal, environmental setup and precision, combined mechanical support, and compounding supernatural pressure must feel different minute to minute.
6. **Different recovery problem:** every faction retrieves, preserves, and restores bodies through a different spatial and institutional process.
7. **Different failure shape:** breaking a colonial port chain, fox relationship network, pirate treasury or prize fleet, scarce elven roster and sanctuary cycle, Michael industrial route, or Cthulhu corruption engine must produce visibly different collapse behavior.
8. **No mirrored roster:** no faction receives a renamed one-to-one equivalent of another faction's nine units or buildings.
9. **No universal optimal order:** a build order, formation, or territorial plan that is correct for one faction should be inefficient or impossible for at least most others.
10. **Conversion stress test:** when a woman transfers to Michael or a dead character rises for Cthulhu, the loss and gain must retain the source faction's unique mechanics rather than flattening the person into a generic unit.

## 19.3 Colonial powers - Occupy

### Core fantasy and strategic verb

The colonial powers turn arrival into administration and administration into permanent positional control.

Their verb is **Occupy**.

They land people and material, establish roads and depots, survey territory, build defensible settlements, impose a chain of command, and convert open ground into firing lanes. Their strength is not merely muskets or artillery. It is the ability to make a captured location progressively harder to contest while supplying disciplined forces through a legible institutional network.

### Population engine

Colonial population is generated through a combination of scheduled arrivals and settlement growth.

- A functioning port, landing office, or protected anchorage admits periodic groups of settlers, soldiers, officials, tradespeople, and specialists.
- Housing, provisions, sanitation, administration, and security determine how many arrivals can remain and how quickly local buildings generate additional workers and trained units.
- Strong roads and convoy routes distribute population from the coast toward interior settlements.
- Hospitals, churches or sanctioned ritual institutions, and civil registries reduce permanent loss by keeping the wounded alive, identifying bodies, and organizing recovery before midnight.

The colonial system can replace basic population efficiently, but it is vulnerable at its entry points. Blockade the port, break the road chain, overload housing, destroy the administrative center, or make the settlement appear unsafe and the population cycle contracts sharply.

### Economy

Colonial priorities are provisions, timber and stone, metal, powder and ammunition, draft animals, medicine, administrative capacity, and secure transport. The economy converts bulk material and organized labor into roads, fortifications, standardized weapons, and trained formations.

### Building cycle

The provisional colonial building family is:

1. Landing office and port - population arrival, customs, naval supply, and strategic connection.
2. Housing block - civilian capacity, workers, and settlement stability.
3. Farm and provision yard - food, animals, storage, and convoy supply.
4. Warehouse and depot - route throughput, reserves, and reinforcement staging.
5. Barracks and drill square - line troops, officers, and formation training.
6. Stable and courier post - mounted scouts, messengers, and dragoons.
7. Engineer yard - sappers, roads, bridges, fieldworks, and repair.
8. Foundry and artillery yard - guns, shot, powder support, and siege equipment.
9. Hospital and sanctioned chapel - injury treatment, morale, body identification, and resurrection support.
10. Administrative and signal house - orders, maps, intelligence, law, and coordinated response.
11. Fort, redoubt, and gun battery - escalating positional defense.

### Nine ordinary unit families

1. Settler-worker - builds, farms, carries, repairs, and staffs civilian structures.
2. Line infantry - disciplined ranged body that gains power from ordered volleys and mutual support.
3. Skirmisher - loose-order scout and marksman for woods, roads, and screening.
4. Dragoon - mounted courier, pursuit unit, mobile reserve, and dismounted firearm troop.
5. Sapper - constructs cover, clears obstacles, breaches walls, repairs routes, and places field charges.
6. Field surgeon and orderly team - stabilizes casualties and organizes evacuation before midnight.
7. Officer and standard bearer team - maintains formation, order transmission, morale, and controlled retreat.
8. Artillery battery - crewed direct or indirect fire that requires ammunition, setup, sight lines, and protection.
9. Garrison guard - close-defense infantry trained for gates, walls, buildings, and settlement security.

### Battlefield doctrine

Colonial forces prefer roads, surveyed approaches, prepared cover, mutually supporting formations, and artillery lanes. They fight best when they can identify the front, deploy deliberately, and retreat through protected stages. They are weaker when surrounded, deprived of command, pulled into fragmented rooms, or forced to pursue elusive enemies through unsurveyed terrain.

Their battlefield readability comes from straight lines, standards, drum or signal cadence, measured volleys, fieldworks appearing during a fight, mounted messengers, and guns whose danger is obvious before they fire.

#### Sacrificial breach commandos

The colonial faction can assemble a specialized breach formation from veteran infantry, sappers, and an officer rather than spawning a tenth generic unit family. These are rough adult men selected or volunteering for hard duty with an understood casualty rate. Their job is narrow and concrete: open or exploit an engineered entrance, enter during the lethal first seconds, kill or displace the nearest defenders, fix a visible route marker inside, and hold the threshold until the following formation passes.

They are armed for one violent close action rather than prolonged line combat: several black-powder fuse grenades, a brace of percussion pistols or cap-and-ball revolvers, and a short sword, hanger, or heavy fighting knife. Grenades are period spherical or early cylindrical bombs carried in reinforced leather loops or canvas satchels; modern segmented grenades, safety levers, tactical pouches, automatic weapons, and contemporary commando silhouettes are outside the visual and mechanical contract.

The grenades are not incidental equipment. Grenade saturation is the formation's opening doctrine. Several fuses are prepared together, grenades pass through the entrance before the bodies do, and the commandos cross during the few seconds in which concussion, smoke, splinters, noise, fear, and broken formation prevent an organized defensive answer. The ideal result is not universal lethality; it is a defended interior temporarily made incapable of behaving like a defended interior.

Crossing that threshold triggers a deliberate short human frenzy. For its duration the breach formation resists fear, pain interruption, ranged suppression, and hesitation; it moves and attacks faster at close range and strongly prefers the marked route and its immediate threats. The same state reduces ranged discipline, peripheral threat response, restraint, and ability to disengage. It is not magical berserking and it does not prevent injury. It is frightened people converting their only plausible chance of survival into overwhelming forward violence before the smoke clears.

They do not wear a dedicated parade uniform. The island heat and confined work lead them to discard outer coats and reduce period garments to shirts with rolled or removed sleeves, open waistcoats, braces, crossbelts, light or shortened trousers, gaiters, and battered boots. Their shared recognition device is a plain red breach marker and guide cord visible through smoke, not identical clothing. Beatrix's fitted interior bodice, cut-down cavalry breeches, and period pinned hair follow the same logic for a female captain without turning the formation into modern raiders wearing historical textures.

The formation is extremely effective against doors, barricades, narrow defenses, and briefly disorganized interiors. It is expensive in trained people, weak in open ground, short on sustained ammunition, and especially dangerous to use when bodies cannot be recovered before midnight. Bad fuse timing, an uncleared friendly, several defensive angles, an endpoint that remains blocked, or defenders able to endure the opening blasts can convert the rush into friendly fire and concentrated casualties. Colonial AI should form and commit it only when the strategic value of the opening exceeds the expected losses, a grenade path and reachable endpoint exist, and a follow-on force is ready to exploit the breach.

### Strategic AI

The colonial AI prioritizes ports, roads, depots, defensible settlements, resource survey, connected territory, formal alliances, and the reduction of uncontrolled spaces. It values holding and improving a smaller connected region over briefly painting distant nodes in its color.

It responds to Michael's recruitment by controlling access to important women, offering status or protection, accelerating replacement training, negotiating from institutional interest, and sometimes treating a departure as a useful political bridge. No response makes a woman immune.

It initially treats Cthulhu as disorder, criminal conspiracy, disease, or enemy sabotage. Discipline and records delay early collapse, but rigid command can spread one corrupted order through an entire formation. Large conventional battles also create exactly the corpse fields Cthulhu wants.

### Recovery and resurrection

Colonial recovery is logistical and institutional. Orderlies tag and evacuate bodies; field surgeons stabilize; hospitals preserve; sanctioned clergy or licensed ritual specialists restore when facilities and records are intact. The system handles many casualties efficiently near roads and settlements but performs poorly behind enemy lines or after administrative collapse.

### Dungeon and loot identity

Colonial dungeons are forts, magazines, prisons, customs houses, buried redoubts, commandeered estates, and military storehouses. Higher tiers add interlocking fire lanes, locked records, powder hazards, disciplined patrol schedules, concealed sally ports, and increasingly valuable standardized arms, maps, medicines, payroll, and strategic documents.

### Strengths, weaknesses, and victory behavior

Strengths are population throughput, disciplined ranged combat, siege power, roads, fortification, casualty administration, and coherent territorial control. Weaknesses are dependence on ports and supply, predictable doctrine, slow redeployment outside prepared routes, institutional brittleness, and high-value concentrated targets.

In a neutral sandbox, the colonial faction attempts to win by establishing a connected governed territory, controlling ports and major roads, neutralizing irregular threats, and forcing rivals into treaties, dependency, expulsion, or defeat.

## 19.4 Fox people - Misdirect

### Core fantasy and strategic verb

The fox people survive through social density, distributed settlements, supernatural misdirection, mobility, and the ability to make enemies act on the wrong understanding of the island.

Their verb is **Misdirect**.

They do not need to hold the strongest wall if the enemy attacks an empty village, pursues a false column, mistakes a festival for weakness, or discovers that the apparently isolated defenders were connected through hidden paths and family networks.

### Population engine

Fox population grows through many small hearths rather than a few dominant centers.

- Houses, family compounds, shrine villages, workshops, markets, and festivals generate workers, fighters, artisans, mediums, and scouts.
- Multiple modest settlements can continue producing people when one major location falls.
- Social bonds, hospitality, kinship, and celebration keep small locations attractive and restore morale after displacement.
- Refugees can be distributed among many hearths instead of overloading one capital.
- Hidden paths let population relocate before a front closes.

This is the most decentralized native population engine. It is difficult to stop with one siege but sensitive to widespread fear, broken trust, corrupted hospitality, and the loss of many small shrines at once.

### Fox wooing of men

Fox women can woo living men from other factions. This is the fox faction's allegiance-growth mechanic and the male-facing counterpart to Michael's ability, but it is structurally decentralized: the relationship belongs to a particular fox woman rather than to the fox leader or faction as an abstract global aura.

A potential recruit must be a living sapient man, reachable through social contact, and capable of choosing a new allegiance. Courtship progress depends on attraction, compatibility, shared experiences, favors, safety, the fox woman's personal influence, the man's current loyalty and relationships, and the political consequences of departure. It is not limited to men with low morale, is not purchased with gold, and cannot affect non-male units, constructs, beasts, or unsuitable undead.

When the relationship succeeds, the man transfers as the same persistent character. He keeps his name, history, equipment where available, source-faction skills, relationships, grudges, hero status, and narrative importance. His attachment is represented through his bond with the fox woman and his place in her household or retinue, giving the fox faction a web of many personal recruitment centers rather than Michael's single central romantic gravity.

Wooed men do not automatically change allegiance again when the fox woman changes faction, dies, is captured, or becomes unavailable. Those events trigger morale, loyalty, rescue, grief, and relationship decisions for the man. In particular, when Michael recruits a fox woman, her wooed men do not enter Michael's faction: Michael still cannot recruit men indirectly. They may remain fox-aligned, rebel, seek her, negotiate contact, or make another individually simulated choice.

### Economy

Fox priorities are food, cloth and crafted goods, medicinal plants, ritual materials, local trade, favors, intelligence, safe paths, and social cohesion. Their economy extracts less bulk material than the colonial or Michael systems but turns distributed local knowledge into mobility and resilience.

### Building cycle

The provisional fox building family is:

1. Hearth house - population, housing, local morale, and civilian work.
2. Family compound - increased capacity, specialist generation, and refugee absorption.
3. Market and festival court - trade, courtship opportunities, relationships, rumor, celebration, and morale recovery.
4. Craft house - clothing, light weapons, tools, traps, and ritual objects.
5. Pathfinder lodge - scouts, couriers, hidden-route discovery, and ambush planning.
6. Blade and bow school - mobile fighters and disciplined skirmishers.
7. Veil shrine - illusions, concealment, counterintelligence, and local magical defense.
8. Ancestor lantern house - identity preservation, madness treatment, body recall, and resurrection support.
9. Beast garden - trained animals, spirit companions, and handlers.
10. Hidden gate - rapid movement between prepared nodes and protected evacuation.
11. Layered village defenses - traps, false entrances, concealed firing positions, and escape routes.

### Nine ordinary unit families

1. Hearth worker - farms, crafts, carries, repairs, and keeps a settlement socially functional.
2. Pathfinder - fast scout, courier, tracker, and hidden-route specialist.
3. Bow skirmisher - mobile ranged unit that fires from concealment and relocates quickly.
4. Blade dancer - evasive close fighter that punishes isolated enemies and broken formations.
5. Veil caster - creates decoys, concealment, false sounds, and misleading local information.
6. Lantern medium - protects identity, detects spiritual corruption, calms madness, and supports recovery.
7. Foxfire runner - fast magical harassment and signal unit that marks routes or targets with visible flame.
8. Beast handler - fields a limited trained animal or spirit companion appropriate to the local kit.
9. Hearth guardian - durable polearm or shield defender who protects civilians, shrines, and withdrawal paths.

### Battlefield doctrine

Fox forces avoid symmetrical collisions. They spread, hide, bait, flank, isolate, and retreat before the enemy can convert contact into a stable front. Their decoys must be rule-governed and readable: careful enemies can detect inconsistencies, follow physical footprints, disrupt veil anchors, or force the fox force to protect a real objective.

Their battlefield readability comes from lanterns, foxfire marks, repeated false silhouettes, sudden lateral movement, hidden routes opening, small groups coordinating from several directions, and guardians appearing only when a real hearth is threatened.

### Strategic AI

The fox AI preserves people and relationships before territory. It prefers distributed growth, information advantage, reversible commitments, hidden reserve routes, selective alliance, and enemies wasting strength against false priorities.

It responds to Michael through social knowledge rather than imprisonment. Communities track his relationships, warn or encourage women according to local interests, demand reciprocity, and may deliberately use a willing recruit as a diplomatic bridge or source of influence. No custom or shrine removes Michael's potential access to any woman. If Michael recruits a fox woman who has wooed men into the fox faction, those men do not transfer with her, although their personal reactions can reshape fox politics and create rescue, rivalry, grief, or alliance stories.

It recognizes Cthulhu through broken stories, missing memories, unnatural dreams, false relatives, and corrupted shrines. Its familiarity with illusion helps identify some deceptions, but Cthulhu can turn a society built on trust and perception against itself. One compromised medium can poison the credibility of true warnings.

### Recovery and resurrection

Fox recovery uses ancestor lanterns, names, personal objects, spirit paths, and teams trained to retrieve the lost. Distributed lantern houses provide many modest recovery points. They are resilient to the loss of one center but individually limited and vulnerable to identity corruption. A body without a correctly preserved name or memory becomes much harder to reclaim after midnight.

### Dungeon and loot identity

Fox dungeons are hidden shrine complexes, folded estates, abandoned festival grounds, spirit paths, trapped storehouses, and layered village interiors whose apparent layout may be false. Higher tiers add multiple valid routes, decoy objectives, social or identity puzzles, spirit guardians, mobile ambushes, rare craftwork, glamours, medicines, maps of hidden paths, and relationship intelligence.

### Strengths, weaknesses, and victory behavior

Strengths are decentralized population, relationship recruitment of men, mobility, concealment, information, morale recovery, evacuation, ambush, and resistance to single-point decapitation. Weaknesses are low siege weight, limited heavy armor, dependence on individual fox women and many social anchors, difficulty holding open ground, romantic and political backlash, and the danger that corrupted information spreads through the same networks that normally protect them.

In a neutral sandbox, the fox faction attempts to win by preserving a wide living network, controlling information and hidden movement, making invasion too expensive, isolating hostile leaders, and arranging alliances or collapses without presenting one decisive target.

## 19.5 Pirates - Raid

### Core fantasy and strategic verb

Pirates convert mobility, gold, dissatisfaction, live capture, salvage, and stolen machinery into temporary local superiority.

Their verb is **Raid**.

They possess a modest native population engine, but successful piracy also creates cash and opportunity that can buy the allegiance of people already dissatisfied with their current faction. They raid both loyalty and machinery: underpaid people can change crews, and badly protected vessels or machines can change owners.

### Population engine

Pirate population has two sources: modest native generation and paid voluntary defection.

- Dockside households, shipboard households, and crew quarters generate general pirate civilians and deckhands rather than completed specialist squads.
- Berths and provisions determine how many existing pirates can serve aboard ships or in mobile shore parties.
- Captains assign people from the faction's own population to temporary billets such as boarder, marksman, rigger, gun crew, lookout, or landing-party surgeon.
- Returning to a suitable anchorage allows surviving crew members to change billets when equipment and training permit.
- Captured ships, cannon, weapons, cargo, and buildings expand capacity and available loadouts but never create the people required to use them.
- A profitable free port improves food security, morale, equipment, household stability, population survival, and the credibility of pirate employment offers.
- Gold, prize shares, escape, protection, and opportunity can recruit dissatisfied living men and women from other factions.
- Pirate loyalty remains conditional. Unfair shares, unpaid promises, pointless danger, hunger, and illegitimate captains can cause recruits and native pirates alike to rebel or desert.

### Paid recruitment contract

Pirate recruitment does not manufacture dissatisfaction and cannot buy absolute loyalty. It finds a grievance that already exists and offers the person a materially better deal.

A candidate must be a living sapient person, reachable by a pirate contact or offer, legally able to change allegiance within the simulation, and dissatisfied enough for an offer to matter. The decision weighs a tunable combination of gold, promised shares, immediate payment, pirate reputation, escape opportunity, personal ties, and expected freedom against faction loyalty, current pay, safety, ideology, family obligations, fear of retaliation, and distrust of the recruiting captain.

If the offer succeeds, the same persistent character transfers to the pirate faction with her or his identity, history, relationships, origin-faction skills, equipment where available, hero status, and grievances intact. The recruit joins under a contract or share agreement rather than permanent romantic loyalty. Pirates must continue meeting enough of the bargain to retain that person.

Michael-aligned women are ineligible regardless of dissatisfaction score, offered gold, captivity, or pirate reputation because women who willingly join Michael do not leave his living faction. Michael has no ordinary male population for pirates to recruit. Pirates may steal Michael's machines and vessels, but they cannot purchase his women away from him.

Living people under Cthulhu influence can only consider a pirate offer after madness control has been sufficiently disrupted; gold alone cannot outbid active supernatural domination. Undead, monsters, automatons, beasts, and non-person units are never recruited through pay.

### Capture doctrine

Pirates prefer to capture sapient enemies when circumstances allow. A captured character remains a persistent member of the original faction but enters a prisoner state controlled by the pirate captor. The source faction loses that character's labor, combat strength, relationships, command effects, and hero powers until release, escape, rescue, exchange, or another resolution.

Prisoners can provide ransom, prisoner exchange, negotiated passage, political leverage, limited intelligence, rescue objectives, and pressure on faction morale. Capture alone does not make them fight for the pirates, fill pirate crew billets, or lose their original relationships. A dissatisfied living prisoner may later accept a genuine paid contract, but this is a separate recruitment decision; captivity supplies contact, not automatic loyalty. Fair treatment can make an offer credible, while cruelty usually increases resistance and damages pirate reputation. Food, transport, guard capacity, and escape security create real costs for holding them.

Capture also changes the Cthulhu economy. A living prisoner does not rise at midnight. Pirates therefore deny Cthulhu bodies whenever they capture instead of kill, sometimes becoming an accidental brake on the supernatural snowball. Executions, deaths in custody, failed prison transports, and slaughter during rescue attempts can reverse that advantage and create concentrated midnight disasters.

### Vessel, machinery, and equipment piracy

Pirates can seize and reuse every practical class of portable equipment, movable vessel, and machinery: weapons, armor, tools, ammunition, field gear, magical implements, sailing ships, steamships, boats, wagons, artillery, cranes, industrial equipment, Michael's steam wagons, walkers, automata, rocket platforms, and airships. They do not learn to manufacture the captured technology merely by owning one example.

Asset piracy follows a physical sequence: isolate the target, suppress or remove its operators, board or reach its controls, defeat locks or self-denial systems, secure it against recapture, extract or tow it, repair or jury-rig it, and assign pirate crew. A prize that cannot be moved before relief arrives may need to be stripped, sabotaged, ransomed back, or abandoned.

Captured equipment and machinery keep their original ammunition, fuel, charges, parts, attunement, control, maintenance, and specialist requirements. Pirates are unusually good at improvising adapters, substituting components, cannibalizing one prize to sustain another, and accepting degraded performance. Ordinary weapons may be reassigned quickly; specialized technology, bonded elven craft, fox ritual objects, and Cthulhu artifacts demand knowledge or impose risk. Capture grants use, sale, ransom, or salvage value; it never unlocks the source faction's production tree.

This makes pirates a hard strategic counter to machine-heavy factions. Every expensive movable asset sent beyond secure support is also a potential pirate prize. Michael is especially exposed because his technology concentrates enormous value in stealable objects. His counterplay is escort, keyed controls, loyal operators, scuttling charges, remote shutdown, rapid recovery, guarded repair routes, and designs that are difficult to jury-rig. These measures raise pirate cost but do not create blanket immunity.

### Economy

Pirate priorities are gold, provisions, drink and morale goods, crew capacity, powder, weapons, sailcloth and rope, repair timber, captured cargo, intelligence, prisoner capacity, safe anchorages, and fair distribution of prize shares. Gold is both purchasing power and a population weapon: spending it on recruitment competes directly with repairs, supplies, bribes, ransom, and crew pay.

### Building cycle

The provisional pirate building family is:

1. Hidden anchorage - boats, movement, concealment, and coastal supply.
2. Dockside quarters and tavern - modest native population generation, paid-recruit contacts, housing, morale, rumors, shares, and social coordination.
3. Prize court, counting house, and brig - loot division, secure detention, ransom, exchanges, morale, legitimacy, and diplomacy.
4. Warehouse and fence - flexible storage, resale, and conversion of captured goods.
5. Ropewalk and sail loft - route speed, ship repair, climbing equipment, and rigging.
6. Gunsmith and powder store - firearms, grenades, ammunition, and dangerous stockpiles.
7. Boarding yard - close fighters, capture equipment, and assault training.
8. Lookout and signal tower - sea lanes, warnings, target selection, and weather observation.
9. Ship surgeon and recovery house - stabilization, body handling, and limited restoration support.
10. Charm house or tide shrine - negotiated supernatural protection and resurrection assistance.
11. Shipyard, drydock, and conversion yard - native vessels, captured-vessel repair, machinery adaptation, heavy guns, mobile bases, and late-game projection.

### Nine ordinary unit families

1. Deckhand - worker, sailor, hauler, repairer, and emergency fighter.
2. Cutlass boarder - aggressive close unit specialized in breaching, disabling, surrender pressure, and live capture.
3. Pistol raider - mobile short-range burst fighter that thrives in broken terrain.
4. Rigging marksman - elevated shooter, climber, lookout, and mast-to-roof mover.
5. Grenadier - area denial, smoke, fire, shock, and dangerous powder handling.
6. Rigger and saboteur - ropes, grapples, nets, restraints, traps, door access, ship repair, control seizure, jury-rigging, and machinery theft.
7. Ship surgeon - stabilization, crude treatment, body preparation, and evacuation.
8. Boatswain and flag team - local command, morale, boarding coordination, surrender windows, prisoner control, and prize discipline.
9. Boarding brute - scarce heavy assault unit whose size and force are useful in a narrow direct role.

### Battlefield doctrine

Pirates choose where a fight begins. Boats, grapples, hidden approaches, smoke, vertical routes, bribed information, and rapid concentration let them create an unfair local encounter, seize the objective, take valuable enemies alive, commandeer machinery, and leave before the enemy's full force arrives. They prefer capture because intact cargo, vessels, weapons, prisoners, machines, and usable buildings are worth more than ruins. Captured people require a separate paid recruitment decision; captured hardware does not.

Their battlefield readability comes from swinging or climbing movement, pistols discharged at close range, smoke, ropes and grapples, sudden flank arrivals, visible loot objectives, and clear withdrawal calls.

### Strategic AI

Pirate AI values gold, profit, freedom of movement, defensible anchorages, famous captains, dissatisfied enemy personnel, weakly guarded machinery, high-leverage prisoners, its own captured people, and fights that can end before attrition consumes the crew. It compares the cost of killing, capturing, bribing, stealing, stripping, ransoming, and withdrawing rather than treating every contact as an extermination battle. It forms alliances readily when useful and abandons them readily when shares, autonomy, or survival are threatened.

It responds to Michael's recruitment personally. A woman's departure may be treated as romance, betrayal, liberation, loss of a valuable officer, settlement of a debt, or an opportunity to gain Michael as a friend. The reaction follows crew relationships and prize politics rather than bureaucracy. Pirates know Michael's women cannot be bought, so their AI redirects recruitment spending toward other factions while evaluating Michael primarily as the island's richest source of stealable machinery.

Pirates often recognize Cthulhu earlier than colonials because sailors encounter impossible water, missing crews, and midnight dead. Recognition does not guarantee cooperation. Some captains flee, some profit from panic, some make bargains, and some organize audacious recovery raids. Pirate casualty rates and abandoned bodies can feed Cthulhu catastrophically when raids fail.

### Recovery and resurrection

Pirate recovery is mobile and improvised. Surgeons stabilize, riggers retrieve bodies with ropes and boats, crews remember personal effects and names, and tide shrines or hired practitioners perform limited restoration. The system can reach dangerous places quickly but lacks the throughput of a colonial hospital, the distribution of fox lanterns, or the regenerative depth of an elven grove.

### Dungeon and loot identity

Pirate dungeons are beached ships, sea caves, hidden ports, prize warehouses, brigs, mutinous forts, wreck fields, and floating strongholds. Higher tiers add rigging routes, flooding, powder chains, trapdoors, vertical ambushes, movable boats, prisoner-rescue or treasure choices, captured faction equipment, rare maps, contraband, and accumulated loot from multiple origins.

### Strengths, weaknesses, and victory behavior

Strengths are mobility, initiative, amphibious access, paid recruitment from dissatisfied populations, nonlethal capture, ransom leverage, machinery piracy, flexible billet assignment, equipment reuse, and rapid local concentration. Weaknesses are gold dependence, conditional loyalty, modest native generation, volatile morale, prisoner-management burdens, incompatible stolen equipment, fragile powder and ships, poor performance in prolonged attrition, and dependence on successful captains and profitable routes.

In a neutral sandbox, pirates attempt to win by controlling sea movement and high-value routes, maintaining prosperous free ports, buying away neglected personnel, breaking monopolies, capturing rather than annihilating useful assets and important enemies, assembling a prize fleet from other factions' hardware, and making every rival bargain or pay for movement through pirate-controlled space.

## 19.6 Elves - Shape

### Core fantasy and strategic verb

Elves turn exceptional magic, craft, hardiness, and living terrain into leverage for a small, very slowly replenished people.

Their verb is **Shape**.

Where colonials impose roads and walls, elves persuade or command the local ecology to become the settlement. A secure grove is housing, food, concealment, healing, craftsmanship, intelligence, and a route network at once. It makes existing elves more capable and difficult to kill, and only over a long protected cycle can it add a small number of new elves.

### Scarce population doctrine

The elven starting population is small. Protected sanctuaries can generate new elves, but the cycle is long, capacity is narrow, and it cannot be accelerated enough to refill an army after a serious defeat.

- Every elf is a persistent strategic asset from the moment she or he enters the campaign.
- Elven structures chiefly house, train, equip, heal, conceal, transport, and strengthen existing elves.
- Only a healthy sanctuary with sufficient territory, safety, time, and magical support can advance the slow generation cycle.
- Losing or corrupting the sanctuary pauses or reverses accumulated generation progress rather than transferring it freely to another building.
- Elves may change professions and battlefield roles through craft, training, and magical equipment, but a role change does not create another body.
- A recovered or resurrected elf is the same preserved person returning and does not consume the much slower replacement cycle.
- A permanently lost elf can eventually be replaced as raw population, but the replacement does not inherit the dead elf's training, relationships, memories, authority, hero status, or specialist value.
- Elves may employ beasts, constructs, summoned effects, living defenses, or allied forces, but those assets cannot perform every elven role and do not remove the population bottleneck.

This makes elven hardiness structural rather than cosmetic. They require superior avoidance, mitigation, withdrawal, healing, rescue, concealment, and resurrection reliability because ordinary attrition outpaces their generation. Their campaign arc is primarily the increasing capability, equipment, magic, territory, and history of a scarce cast, with slow population recovery preventing one bad battle from being literally irreversible while ensuring that it remains consequential for a long time.

### Economy

Elven priorities are healthy territory, water, light, living materials, magical essence, rare craft inputs, beasts, memory, sanctuary capacity, and uninterrupted time. They require fewer bulk goods than colonial or industrial factions but cannot convert a sudden resource surplus into rapid replacement population.

### Building cycle

The provisional elven building family is:

1. Living heart - strategic core, territorial health, magic distribution, and preservation anchor.
2. Sanctuary and bower - very slow capped population generation, housing, healing, concealment, education, and role retraining.
3. Cultivation terrace - food, medicine, fibers, and living material.
4. Sapworks - bows, light armor, tools, resins, and shaped equipment.
5. Ranger lodge - scouts, archers, patrols, and long-range observation.
6. Blade grove - mobile melee fighters and guardian training.
7. Beast hollow - animal companions, mounts, handlers, and ecological reconnaissance.
8. Spell circle - terrain shaping, wards, weather moderation, and magical support.
9. Rebirth grove - recovery and return of the same existing identity, cleansing, and resurrection; much faster than replacing raw headcount through the sanctuary cycle.
10. Rootway gate - rapid movement, evacuation, and supply through healthy territory.
11. Living bastion - roots, thorns, elevated firing positions, and regenerating defense.

### Nine ordinary unit families

1. Cultivator - worker, healer of terrain, builder, harvester, and grove tender.
2. Longbow sentinel - precise long-range fighter strongest with sight lines and elevation.
3. Blade runner - fast melee interceptor that travels through shaped terrain.
4. Root guardian - durable defender that anchors to terrain and protects a local position.
5. Grove shaper - creates cover, thorns, ramps, roots, bridges, and temporary route changes.
6. Spell warden - wards allies, counters corruption, and moderates dangerous local magic or weather.
7. Canopy scout - high-mobility observer using trees, cliffs, and vertical routes.
8. Beast rider or handler - fast flanking, pursuit, transport, and bond-based animal support.
9. Rebirth attendant - stabilizes bodies, preserves identity, cleanses corruption, and operates recovery rituals.

### Battlefield doctrine

Elves change the room before winning inside it. They create cover, deny paths with roots and thorns, open elevated or hidden routes, establish long sight lines, and regenerate while the shaped terrain remains connected. Their units are individually capable but receive much of their advantage from the environment they prepared.

Their battlefield readability comes from visible roots, growing barriers, luminous path lines, marked shooting perches, living cover, beasts using newly opened routes, and environmental effects that have physical anchors enemies can destroy.

### Strategic AI

Elven AI values survival of every elf, healthy connected biomes, water, magical sites, long-term territory quality, protected sanctuaries, uninterrupted generation progress, reliable retreat paths, and avoidance of wars whose expected losses exceed what their slow replacement cycle can repair. It may surrender a built node while making the surrounding region unusable to the occupier, or spend great power recovering one endangered elf whose accumulated skills exceed the local military value of the territory.

It responds to Michael's recruitment through the woman's place in the living network. Her departure removes real knowledge, magic, relationships, and sometimes stewardship of a place. Some departures are treated as wounds; others create bridges through which Michael's technology and elven cultivation can cooperate. No bond to a grove makes a woman immune.

Elves recognize Cthulhu through ecological wrongness, dead zones, corrupted rebirth, and weather that stops serving living cycles. Their connected terrain gives early information and powerful cleansing, but also gives corruption a network through which to spread. A captured living heart can become a major Cthulhu multiplier.

### Recovery and resurrection

Elven recovery depends on rebirth groves, living memory, preserved identity, and a viable path between body and network. It is exceptionally reliable while the ecology is healthy because ordinary replacement is extremely slow and restores only raw headcount. Recovery becomes unreliable or dangerous when corruption reaches the grove, and a corrupted rebirth site can return a person incorrectly or deliver the dead directly into Cthulhu's influence. Successful resurrection preserves the actual experienced character rather than waiting for a new novice to emerge from the sanctuary cycle.

### Dungeon and loot identity

Elven dungeons are root labyrinths, hollow living towers, sealed groves, vertical canopy settlements, submerged gardens, and corrupted rebirth sites. Higher tiers add changing organic routes, regeneration, line-of-sight puzzles, awakened defenders, weather interaction, living locks, rare seeds, magical fibers, medicines, bonded creatures, memory objects, and terrain-shaping artifacts.

### Strengths, weaknesses, and victory behavior

Strengths are exceptional individual quality, magic, craft, hardiness, terrain shaping, precision range, recovery, ecological intelligence, healthy-territory mobility, and compounding local defense. Weaknesses are extremely slow population replacement, irreplaceable accumulated experience, fire, blight, salt, corruption, dependence on linked living terrain, slower projection into dead ground, and catastrophic recovery time when several elves or a central sanctuary are lost together.

In a neutral sandbox, elves attempt to win by preserving their scarce people while connecting enough healthy territory that the island's movement, food, water, recovery, craft, and magic depend upon their living network, then containing or transforming rivals without paying an attritional price their slow generation cannot repair in time.

## 19.8 Why population systems are not sameness

- Colonials grow through ports, administration, housing, and scheduled settlement throughput.
- Fox people grow through distributed hearths and through individual fox women wooing living men into personal households and the faction.
- Pirates combine modest dockside generation with gold-and-grievance recruitment of dissatisfied living people; they also steal movable machinery without recruiting its former operators.
- Elves grow only through an exceptionally slow sanctuary cycle and rely primarily on magic, craft, hardiness, terrain, recovery, and resurrection to preserve scarce experienced people.
- Michael manufactures machines and recruits women already present in the world.
- Cthulhu converts the living, claims unresolved dead, and creates non-person forces.

The result is six different answers to the same strategic question: where does the next worker or fighter come from, and what can an enemy do to prevent that person from becoming available?

It must also produce six different answers to movement, territory, battle, recovery, and victory. Population asymmetry alone is insufficient. Complete mechanical difference is a release requirement for the faction layer.

## 19.9 Balance controls and anti-snowball rules

Population production is a compensator, not a complete defense.

- Basic replacements arrive faster than replacement specialists or heroes.
- Training buildings, equipment, morale, and leadership constrain military conversion.
- Housing and provisions cap sustainable population.
- Repeated losses reduce training quality and institutional memory even when headcount recovers.
- Michael's recruitment remains limited by contact, relationships, conditions, travel, quests, opportunity, and the player's attention rather than immunity.
- Fox recruitment is limited by the number, location, relationships, and personal capacity of fox women; losing or recruiting away a fox woman removes a recruitment center without automatically transferring the men attached to her.
- Pirate recruitment requires existing dissatisfaction, physical or social contact, sufficient gold or shares, a credible offer, and continued fulfillment. It cannot affect Michael-aligned women and competes with every other use of pirate wealth.
- Pirate machinery capture requires physical seizure, extraction, crew, adaptation, fuel, ammunition, parts, and maintenance. A stolen machine does not unlock manufacture or become self-sustaining.
- Elves receive only slow, capped, sanctuary-dependent replacement population. Their neutral viability must come from superior survival, recovery, magic, craft, and force multiplication rather than a hidden emergency spawn multiplier.
- Cthulhu can convert faster as despair, corruption, casualties, and time rise, but early neutral balance must still permit containment.
- Body-recovery capacity is finite and spatial. A faction winning a battle far from its recovery network may still feed Cthulhu.
- A faction with many people creates more strategic depth and more potential Cthulhu corpses; raw population is not automatically safety.
- Hero loss removes party and strategic passives even if an ordinary replacement is generated immediately.

## 19.10 Required simulation tests

Before numerical approval, the faction chassis must be tested across repeated seeded simulations with identical island graphs.

Required comparisons include:

1. Neutral six-faction sandbox without main-scenario Cthulhu assistance.
2. Main-scenario escalation with growing Cthulhu weather, corruption, madness, and weighted events.
3. Michael recruiting frequently from every faction containing women, including repeated losses that outpace the extremely slow elven sanctuary cycle.
4. Michael recruiting selectively for a few high-value heroes instead of many ordinary women.
5. Fox women wooing men from high-loyalty, low-loyalty, hero, specialist, and geographically isolated populations.
6. Pirates spending gold to recruit dissatisfied people versus spending the same wealth on ships, repairs, bribes, and supplies.
7. Pirates attempting to capture and sustain colonial artillery, elven crafted assets, and Michael machinery under guarded and overextended conditions.
8. High ordinary-faction warfare producing large midnight corpse transfers.
9. Strong alliances that suppress casualty production.
10. Loss of each faction's principal population, recruitment, preservation, or recovery infrastructure.
11. Elven campaigns with low, moderate, and catastrophic attrition, testing whether slow sanctuary generation permits eventual recovery without functioning as an emergency recharge.
12. Coastal, inland, forested, corrupted, and route-fragmented starting positions.
13. Faction elimination and territory redistribution.

Track at least population by sex and class, trained military share, hero survival, women recruited by Michael, men wooed by fox women, people hired by pirates, pirate recruitment spending, unpaid pirate obligations, captured and retained machinery by source faction, living characters converted by madness, unresolved dead raised at midnight, elven losses, elven sanctuary-cycle progress and replacement time, recovery throughput, controlled nodes, functioning population buildings, food and housing pressure, alliances, and time to irreversible collapse.

Do not force equal win percentages before observing why factions win. First require that every win and collapse can be explained through its faction logic and the visible board. Then tune costs, rates, capacities, and AI priorities until no faction is structurally dependent on scenario rescue.

# 20. Accepted decisions

- Pirate Island is an autonomous RTS world experienced through a character-scale RPG.
- The island is a complex network graph, not three lanes.
- One node represents one playable room.
- The six factions are the Asian fox people, colonial powers, pirates, elves, Cthulhu, and Captain Michael's created faction.
- Captain Michael begins alone as a shipwreck survivor.
- His faction has two defining advantages: advanced alternate-1870s technology and the ability to recruit adult women from every faction.
- Michael's machinery includes steam wagons, animal-form automata, walkers, rockets, airships, and related industrial systems.
- Michael's faction operates mostly automatically and accepts simple strategic direction.
- Colonial, fox, pirate, and elven buildings generate ordinary men and women as persistent RTS units through different native population cycles. Michael is the organic-generation exception; elven generation is exceptionally slow and tightly limited.
- Generated people have names and persistent lightweight backstories.
- Some units are heroes and are significantly stronger or more important than ordinary units.
- Authored notables and generated units coexist in one population system.
- Any adult female character is potentially recruitable by Michael under achievable circumstances.
- No female character is permanently protected or immune from Michael.
- Male, non-female, and non-person units are outside Michael's recruitment ability.
- Recruitment transfers an actual unit and weakens the source faction accordingly.
- The diplomatic result can be positive or negative depending on the woman and circumstances.
- Once a woman joins Michael, she does not defect to another living faction.
- Michael can have four women in his main harem, producing a five-character principal party.

# 25. Compact authority statement

Pirate Island is a dynamic autonomous RTS war experienced through Captain Michael and a main party containing up to four women drawn from the island's ordinary population. The five established factions build, spawn, fight, ally, rebel, recover, expand, and pursue strategic goals across a complex graph of playable rooms. Captain Michael begins alone and creates a sixth faction through two defining powers: an extraordinary alternate-1870s industrial technology base and the ability to recruit any adult female character under achievable conditions. No woman is permanently immune; males and non-female or non-person units lie outside his recruitment ability. Recruited women permanently remain Michael-aligned while alive. Any four may occupy his main harem and party, including normal generated units, heroes, major notables, or suitable undead women, and candidates are deliberately unequal.

Where faction rules permit generation, buildings introduce named persistent units with lightweight histories, while hero units and authored notables receive greater power and content. Colonial, fox, pirate, and elven buildings generate people through different cycles; Michael begins with no generated organic population, while elven generation is exceptionally slow, capped, and sanctuary-dependent. Major women can receive extensive authored romance quests, and normal generated women can receive smaller procedural relationship quests. Recruitment always transfers a real existing character and may improve or damage diplomacy depending on the recruiter, target, relationship, offer, and circumstances.

Cthulhu converts living men and women statistically through madness and acquires unresolved dead deterministically at midnight. Other factions may restore their own or allied people through machinery, ritual, magic, medicine, or other faction-appropriate systems. Michael's dead women rise for Cthulhu if not restored, yet continue to love him and are comparatively easy for him to reacquire through deliberate recovery. The main scenario uses madness, midnight resurrection, corruption, weather, and weighted events to make Cthulhu increasingly powerful, causing faction AI to recognize the common threat gradually from evidence rather than through a forced universal alliance. Every faction must remain a complete, distinctive RTS power with an obvious battlefield niche rather than a visual reskin of the same army.

The provisional ordinary-faction chassis give colonials the play of mass settlement, occupation, ports, roads, formations, and administration; fox people the play of distributed hearths, fox women wooing men, social intelligence, deception, and hidden movement; pirates the play of gold-and-grievance recruitment, conditional contracts, live capture, ransom, flexible crew billets, and piracy of every practical vessel, machine, and useful piece of equipment; and elves the play of a small, slowly replenished population amplified by exceptional magic, craft, hardiness, preservation, and living terrain. Pirates cannot buy Michael-aligned women, and capturing a prisoner never by itself changes allegiance. Fox men recruited through individual women do not automatically follow those women into Michael's faction. Elven sanctuaries can generate new units, but never quickly enough to erase serious attrition or restore the identity of a lost veteran. Complete mechanical difference is mandatory across population, recruitment, movement, territory, equipment, battle, recovery, collapse, and victory.
